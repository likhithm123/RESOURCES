# UPSC From Zero to IAS - Exam, Roadmap and Strategy
Start here. This notebook explains what the Civil Services Examination (CSE) is, who can write it, how marks work, what to read, and how a Class 12 student or graduate should plan each year. Read it fully once before opening any subject notebook.
## What is UPSC and the Civil Services Examination
- **UPSC** = Union Public Service Commission, a **constitutional body** under **Articles 315-323** (Part XIV). It conducts the CSE every year to recruit officers for about 20+ services.
- **All India Services**: IAS (Indian Administrative Service), IPS (Indian Police Service), IFoS (Indian Forest Service - separate Mains, common Prelims).
- **Central Services Group A**: IFS (Foreign Service), IRS (Income Tax), IRS (Customs and Indirect Taxes), IA&AS (Audit and Accounts), IRAS, IDAS, IPoS (Postal), IIS (Information), ITS (Trade) etc. **Group B**: DANICS, DANIPS, Pondicherry services, AFHQ.
- An IAS officer starts as SDM/Assistant Collector, becomes District Collector/DM in about 5-7 years, later Secretary to State/Union Government; the top post is **Cabinet Secretary**.
Analogy: Think of the exam as three gates. Gate 1 (Prelims) only filters. Gate 2 (Mains) and Gate 3 (Interview) decide your rank.
@chain Notification (Jan-Feb) -> Prelims (May-June) -> Mains (Sept) -> Interview (Jan-Apr) -> Final result -> LBSNAA training :: One exam cycle takes about 12-15 months
## Eligibility
| Item | Rule (always re-check the year's notification) |
|---|---|
| Nationality | IAS, IPS, IFS: citizen of India |
| Education | Bachelor's degree in any stream from a recognised university; final-year students can write Prelims |
| Age | 21 to 32 years on 1 August of exam year (General/EWS) |
| Age relaxation | OBC +3 (35), SC/ST +5 (37), PwBD +10, ex-servicemen as notified |
| Attempts | General/EWS 6, OBC 9, SC/ST unlimited till age limit, PwBD (Gen/OBC) 9 |
- An attempt is counted only if you **appear in any one paper of Prelims**. Applying and not appearing is not an attempt.
- A Class 12 student therefore has 3-4 years of graduation to prepare slowly and deeply. That is a huge advantage.
> You cannot write CSE right after Class 12. Choose any graduation you can genuinely enjoy and score in; choose a degree that also gives you a backup career.
## Stage 1 - Preliminary Examination
| Paper | Questions | Marks | Time | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GS Paper I | 100 | 200 | 2 hours | Counted for cut-off |
| GS Paper II (CSAT) | 80 | 200 | 2 hours | Qualifying: 33% = 66 marks |
- **Negative marking**: one-third of the marks of that question. GS: +2 / -0.66. CSAT: +2.5 / -0.83.
- Prelims marks are **not** added to final merit. About 10-13 lakh apply, 5-6 lakh appear, around 13,000-15,000 qualify for Mains (roughly 12-13 times the vacancies).
- GS cut-off (General) has ranged roughly between 75 and 116 out of 200 in the last decade. Safe target: **110+**.
### GS Paper I syllabus
1. Current events of national and international importance.
2. History of India and Indian National Movement.
3. Indian and World Geography.
4. Indian Polity and Governance.
5. Economic and Social Development.
6. Environmental ecology, biodiversity and climate change.
7. General Science.
### Problem with answer - calculating a Prelims score
Example: A candidate attempts 86 questions in GS-I, gets 58 right and 28 wrong.
Solution: Marks = 58 x 2 - 28 x 0.66 = 116 - 18.48 = **97.52**. If she had left 10 pure guesses (say 3 right, 7 wrong) the score would be 110 - 13.86 = 96.14. So intelligent guessing after eliminating 2 options is worth it; blind guessing is not.
Rule: Eliminate 2 options -> attempt (expected gain +0.67). Eliminate 1 -> usually attempt. Eliminate 0 -> leave.
## Stage 2 - Main Examination
| Paper | Subject | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Paper A | Indian language (qualifying, 25%) | 300 |
| Paper B | English (qualifying, 25%) | 300 |
| Paper I | Essay (2 essays, 125 each) | 250 |
| Paper II | GS I - Heritage, History, Geography, Society | 250 |
| Paper III | GS II - Polity, Governance, Social Justice, IR | 250 |
| Paper IV | GS III - Economy, Agri, S&T, Environment, Security, DM | 250 |
| Paper V | GS IV - Ethics, Integrity, Aptitude | 250 |
| Paper VI | Optional Paper 1 | 250 |
| Paper VII | Optional Paper 2 | 250 |
| | Written total | 1750 |
| | Personality Test | 275 |
| | Grand total | 2025 |
- Each GS paper: 20 questions in 3 hours (10 of 10 marks = 150 words, 10 of 15 marks = 250 words). That is about 9 minutes per question.
- GS IV: Section A theory (125) + Section B six case studies (125).
- Paper A is not compulsory for candidates from Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland and Sikkim.
- Typical final cut-off (General) is about 950-1000 of 2025. Toppers score around 1050-1100, that is only about 52-54%. You do not need perfection; you need consistency across papers.
### Where the marks come from - a realistic target
| Component | Maximum | Good score |
|---|---|---|

| GS I | 250 | 100-110 |
| GS II | 250 | 100-110 |
| GS III | 250 | 95-105 |

> Optional, Essay and Ethics are the three highest-return areas. A student who ignores them for GS facts rarely gets IAS.
## Stage 3 - Personality Test
- 275 marks, about 25-35 minutes, a board of 5 (Chairperson + 4 members). No minimum qualifying marks.
- Based on your **Detailed Application Form (DAF)**: home state, education, hobbies, work, service preferences.
- Tests mental alertness, clear logical exposition, balance of judgement, variety and depth of interest, social cohesion, leadership, intellectual and moral integrity. It is **not** a quiz.
## Service and Cadre Allocation
- Service is given by rank + category + preference + vacancies. In most years IAS for General category closes around rank 75-100; for OBC around 350-450; SC/ST around 500-700.
- Cadre policy 2017: states grouped into **5 zones**; candidates rank zones and cadres. Home cadre depends on vacancy and rank.
## How Questions Are Really Asked
- **Prelims** tests recognition + elimination: statements like "How many of the above are correct?" demand exact knowledge.
- **Mains** tests analysis: directive words decide the answer.
| Directive | What you must do |
|---|---|
| Discuss | cover all sides with arguments |
| Critically examine | positives + negatives + your balanced judgement |
| Analyse | break into parts, show cause-effect |
| Comment | give your view backed by reasons |
| Elucidate / Explain | make clear with examples |
| Evaluate | judge worth using evidence |
| Enumerate | list points briefly |

## Common Mistakes of Beginners
- Collecting material instead of reading it. Limit sources.
- Ignoring CSAT: every year thousands with high GS scores fail CSAT's 66 marks.
- No answer writing until after Prelims. Start by month 4.
- Neglecting the optional subject, which is 500 marks.
- Reading the newspaper for 3 hours. 45 minutes is enough.
- No revision. Without revision, 70% is forgotten in a month.
## Important Questions and Answers
Q: Can a student from MPC/BiPC/Commerce clear UPSC?
A: Yes. The GS syllabus is general; a large share of selected candidates are engineers and doctors. They simply have to build History, Polity and Economy from NCERTs.
Q: Is coaching compulsory?
A: No. The syllabus, PYQs, standard books and a test series are sufficient. Coaching only gives structure; many toppers self-studied.
Q: Which optional should I choose?
A: Choose by (1) genuine interest, (2) availability of material and guidance, (3) overlap with GS (PSIR, Sociology, Geography, History, Public Administration, Anthropology), (4) your graduation background. Read the syllabus and 3 years of papers before deciding.
Q: English medium or regional language?
A: Mains and Interview can be given in any Eighth Schedule language. Choose the language in which you think and write fastest.
Q: How many hours are needed?
A: Quality beats hours. 6 focused hours with college, 8-10 hours full time, for 12-18 months is the common pattern.
Q: What is the difference between IAS and IPS in training?
A: Both do the Foundation Course at LBSNAA, Mussoorie. IAS continues at LBSNAA; IPS trains at SVPNPA, Hyderabad; IFS at SSIFS, New Delhi; IRS (IT) at NADT, Nagpur.
