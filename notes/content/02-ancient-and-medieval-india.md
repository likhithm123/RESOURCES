# History I - Ancient and Medieval India
History is not a list of kings. It is the story of how people lived, produced food, organised power and expressed belief. For every period ask five questions: **Polity, Economy, Society, Religion, Art**. (Mnemonic: PESRA.)
## Sources of History
- **Archaeological**: tools, pottery, coins (numismatics), inscriptions (epigraphy), monuments. **Literary**: religious (Vedas, Tripitakas, Agamas), secular (Arthashastra, Indica), foreign accounts (Megasthenes, Fa-Hien, Hiuen Tsang, Al-Biruni, Ibn Battuta).
- **James Prinsep** deciphered Brahmi and Kharosthi in 1837, which unlocked Ashoka's edicts.
## Prehistoric India
| Age | Period (approx.) | Features | Sites |
|---|---|---|---|
| Palaeolithic | 2 million - 10,000 BCE | hunters, gatherers, hand-axes, fire | Bhimbetka, Soan valley, Attirampakkam, Hunsgi |
| Mesolithic | 10,000 - 6,000 BCE | microliths, first domestication, rock art | Bagor, Adamgarh, Bhimbetka |
| Neolithic | 7,000 - 1,000 BCE | farming, pottery, polished tools, villages | Mehrgarh, Burzahom (pit dwellings), Chirand, Koldihwa (rice) |
| Chalcolithic | 3,000 - 700 BCE | copper + stone, rural cultures | Ahar, Malwa, Jorwe, Inamgaon |
Example: Burzahom in Kashmir shows people living in pits and burying dogs with their masters - a classic Prelims fact.
## Indus Valley (Harappan) Civilisation
- Mature phase about **2600-1900 BCE**; Bronze Age; first urbanisation of India. Discovered 1921 (Harappa, Daya Ram Sahni) and 1922 (Mohenjo-daro, R.D. Banerji). John Marshall headed ASI.
| Site | River / State | Known for |
|---|---|---|
| Harappa | Ravi, Pakistan | granaries, coffin burial |
| Mohenjo-daro | Indus, Pakistan | Great Bath, bronze dancing girl, priest-king |
| Dholavira | Kutch, Gujarat | water reservoirs, three-part city, signboard; UNESCO site 2021 |
| Lothal | Bhogava, Gujarat | dockyard, bead factory, rice husk |
| Kalibangan | Ghaggar, Rajasthan | ploughed field, fire altars |
| Rakhigarhi | Haryana | largest Indian site, DNA study |
| Banawali | Haryana | barley, toy plough |
| Surkotada | Gujarat | horse bones (debated) |
- **Town planning**: grid pattern, citadel and lower town, burnt bricks (ratio 1:2:4), covered drains, no palaces or grand temples.
- **Economy**: wheat, barley, cotton (first in the world); trade with Mesopotamia (Meluhha); standard weights in multiples of 16; seals of steatite; no iron, no coined money.
- **Religion**: mother goddess, Pashupati seal, pipal, humped bull; script pictographic, boustrophedon, **undeciphered**.
- **Decline**: climate change, drying of Ghaggar-Hakra, floods, decline of trade - gradual, not one invasion.
Trap: Harappans did not know iron and the horse was not central. Cow is not shown on seals; unicorn is the most common motif.
## Vedic Age
| Feature | Early / Rig Vedic (1500-1000 BCE) | Later Vedic (1000-600 BCE) |
|---|---|---|
| Region | Sapta Sindhu (Punjab) | Ganga-Yamuna doab |
| Economy | pastoral, cattle = wealth | agriculture, iron (Painted Grey Ware) |
| Polity | tribal: Sabha, Samiti, Vidatha; raja not hereditary absolute | large kingdoms, taxes (bali, bhaga), rituals like Ashvamedha |
| Society | flexible varna, women attended assemblies | rigid four varnas, gotra, ashramas |
| Gods | Indra, Agni, Varuna | Prajapati, Vishnu, Rudra |
- Literature: four **Vedas** (Rig, Sama, Yajur, Atharva) -> Brahmanas -> Aranyakas -> **Upanishads** (philosophy; "Satyameva Jayate" from Mundaka Upanishad).
- Battle of Ten Kings (Dasarajna) on river Parushni (Ravi).
## Mahajanapadas and Rise of Magadha
- 6th century BCE: 16 Mahajanapadas (Anguttara Nikaya), second urbanisation, punch-marked coins, Northern Black Polished Ware.
- **Why Magadha won**: iron ore of Chotanagpur, fertile Ganga plain, elephants, strategic capitals (Rajgir with five hills, Pataliputra at river confluence), ambitious rulers.
@chain Haryanka (Bimbisara, Ajatashatru) -> Shishunaga -> Nanda (Mahapadma) -> Maurya (321 BCE) :: Magadha dynasties
## Buddhism and Jainism
| Point | Buddhism | Jainism |
|---|---|---|
| Founder | Gautama Buddha (Lumbini, 563 BCE trad.) | 24 Tirthankaras; Rishabha first, Mahavira 24th (Kundagrama) |
| Core | Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path, Middle Path | Triratna, five vows, anekantavada, extreme ahimsa |
| Soul / God | no soul (anatta), silent on God | soul in everything, no creator God |
| Texts | Tripitaka in Pali (Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma) | Agamas in Prakrit (Ardhamagadhi) |
| Sects | Hinayana, Mahayana, Vajrayana | Digambara, Shvetambara |
- Buddha's life events: birth (lotus), renunciation (horse), enlightenment at Bodh Gaya (bodhi tree), first sermon at Sarnath (wheel - Dhammachakra pravartana), death at Kushinagar (stupa - Mahaparinirvana).
| Buddhist Council | Place | Patron | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st (483 BCE) | Rajgir | Ajatashatru | Vinaya and Sutta compiled |
| 2nd | Vaishali | Kalashoka | split: Sthaviravadins, Mahasanghikas |
| 3rd | Pataliputra | Ashoka | Abhidhamma; missions sent |
| 4th | Kashmir | Kanishka | Hinayana - Mahayana split |
- Why they rose: reaction to costly rituals and Brahmin dominance, support of Vaishyas (trade needed peace), use of people's languages.
## Mauryan Empire (321-185 BCE)
- **Chandragupta Maurya** with Chanakya (Kautilya, author of **Arthashastra**) overthrew the Nandas; defeated Seleucus (305 BCE). Megasthenes wrote **Indica**. Chandragupta embraced Jainism, died at Shravanabelagola.
- **Bindusara** -> **Ashoka** (268-232 BCE). **Kalinga war** (261 BCE, Rock Edict XIII) changed him to **Dhamma** - a moral code of tolerance, non-violence, respect to elders, welfare; not a religion.
- Edicts: 14 Major Rock Edicts, Pillar Edicts, Minor Rock Edicts; mostly Brahmi script, Prakrit language; Kharosthi in north-west; Greek and Aramaic in Afghanistan. Name "Ashoka" appears at Maski.
- **Administration**: highly centralised; provinces under princes; officials - Amatyas, Dhamma Mahamatras, Rajukas; spies; land tax 1/6; Sudarshana lake in Gujarat.
- **Art**: polished monolithic pillars (Sarnath Lion Capital = national emblem), stupas (Sanchi), Barabar caves for Ajivikas.
- Decline: weak successors, financial strain, huge empire, Pushyamitra Shunga killed Brihadratha in 185 BCE.
## Post-Mauryan Period
- **Indo-Greeks**: first gold coins in India; Menander (Milinda Panha). **Shakas**: Rudradaman's Junagadh inscription (first long Sanskrit inscription). **Kushanas**: Kanishka (Shaka era 78 CE), Silk Route trade, largest gold coin issues; patron of Ashvaghosha, Charaka.
- **Satavahanas** (Deccan): Gautamiputra Satakarni; land grants to Brahmins; lead coins; Amaravati art.
| Art school | Region | Material | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gandhara | NW (Taxila) | grey schist | Greco-Roman, wavy hair, muscular Buddha |
| Mathura | UP | red sandstone | indigenous, smiling Buddha, also Jain and Hindu images |
| Amaravati | Andhra | white marble | narrative panels, dynamic |
## Sangam Age (South India)
- Three kingdoms: **Cheras** (bow, Vanji), **Cholas** (tiger, Uraiyur/Puhar; Karikala built Kallanai), **Pandyas** (fish, Madurai). Sangam literature: Tolkappiyam (grammar), Ettuthokai, Pattupattu, epics Silappadikaram and Manimekalai. Five tinais (landscapes). Roman trade via Muziris and Arikamedu.
## Gupta Empire (c. 319-550 CE)
- Chandragupta I -> **Samudragupta** (Allahabad pillar inscription by Harisena; "Napoleon of India" by V.A. Smith) -> **Chandragupta II Vikramaditya** (Navaratnas, Fa-Hien's visit, Mehrauli iron pillar) -> Kumaragupta (Nalanda) -> Skandagupta (repelled Hunas).
- Features: decentralised administration, land grants (seed of feudalism), gold coins (dinaras) but declining long-distance trade, rise of Bhagavatism, position of women declined (first sati evidence at Eran, 510 CE).
- **Culture**: Kalidasa (Abhijnanashakuntalam, Meghaduta), Aryabhata (zero, pi, earth rotates), Varahamihira, Sushruta tradition, Ajanta paintings, Dashavatara temple at Deogarh (early Nagara), Sanskrit as court language.
- "Golden Age" is true for art and science; critically, it was not golden for lower castes and women.
## Post-Gupta and Early Medieval Kingdoms
- **Harshavardhana** (606-647, Kanauj): Hiuen Tsang visited; Banabhatta wrote Harshacharita; defeated by **Pulakeshin II** (Chalukya, Aihole inscription) on the Narmada.
- **Pallavas** (Kanchi): Mahendravarman, Narasimhavarman (Mamallapuram rathas, Shore Temple) - start of Dravida style.
- **Chalukyas** of Badami: Vesara experiments at Aihole, Pattadakal. **Rashtrakutas**: Kailasa temple at Ellora (Krishna I), Amoghavarsha (Kavirajamarga).
- **Tripartite struggle** for Kanauj: Palas (Bengal; Dharmapala founded Vikramshila), Pratiharas, Rashtrakutas.
### Imperial Cholas (9th-13th century)
- Rajaraja I (Brihadeshwara temple, Thanjavur), Rajendra I (Gangaikondacholapuram; naval expedition to Srivijaya 1025).
- **Local self-government**: Ur, Sabha, Nagaram; **Uttaramerur inscription** describes election by lot (kudavolai) - often cited as early democracy.
- Chola bronzes (Nataraja) made by lost-wax technique.
## Medieval India - Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526)
- Background: Muhammad bin Qasim took Sindh (712); Mahmud of Ghazni raids (Somnath 1025); Muhammad Ghori defeated Prithviraj Chauhan at 2nd Tarain (1192).
| Dynasty | Period | Key rulers and facts |
|---|---|---|
| Slave / Mamluk | 1206-90 | Aibak (Qutub Minar begun); Iltutmish (iqta system, silver tanka, Chalisa); Razia; Balban (blood and iron, sijda) |
| Khalji | 1290-1320 | Alauddin: market control, standing army, dagh and chehra, Malik Kafur's southern campaigns |
| Tughlaq | 1320-1414 | Muhammad bin Tughlaq: capital shift to Daulatabad, token currency; Firoz Shah: canals, jizya on Brahmins; Timur invaded 1398 |
| Sayyid | 1414-51 | Khizr Khan |
| Lodi | 1451-1526 | Sikandar Lodi founded Agra; Ibrahim Lodi lost 1st Panipat (1526) |
- **Iqta system**: land assignments to nobles in lieu of salary. Persian became court language; Indo-Islamic architecture used arch, dome, minaret (Qutub complex, Alai Darwaza).
Case study: Muhammad bin Tughlaq's token currency failed because the state did not hold monopoly over minting - every house became a mint. Lesson for Mains: a good idea fails without implementation capacity.
## Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdoms
- **Vijayanagara** (1336, Harihara and Bukka, capital Hampi): Krishnadevaraya (1509-29, Amuktamalyada, Ashtadiggajas); Nayankara and Ayagar systems; visitors Nicolo Conti, Abdur Razzaq, Domingo Paes; defeat at **Talikota (1565)**.
- **Bahmani** (1347, Gulbarga then Bidar): Mahmud Gawan; broke into five Deccan sultanates - Bijapur (Gol Gumbaz), Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Berar, Bidar.
## Mughal Empire
| Ruler | Period | Remember |
|---|---|---|
| Babur | 1526-30 | 1st Panipat (1526), Khanwa (1527), gunpowder, Baburnama (Turki) |
| Humayun | 1530-56 | lost to Sher Shah at Chausa, Kanauj |
| Sher Shah Suri | 1540-45 | rupiya, Grand Trunk Road, sarais, land measurement |
| Akbar | 1556-1605 | 2nd Panipat (1556), Mansabdari, Todar Mal's Zabt/Dahsala, Din-i-Ilahi (1582), Ibadat Khana, abolished jizya, Fatehpur Sikri |
| Jahangir | 1605-27 | chain of justice, painting at its peak, Thomas Roe, Guru Arjan executed |
| Shah Jahan | 1628-58 | Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Peacock Throne |
| Aurangzeb | 1658-1707 | largest extent, reimposed jizya, Deccan wars, Guru Tegh Bahadur executed |
- **Mansabdari**: every officer held a rank with *zat* (personal status, pay) and *sawar* (cavalry to maintain). **Jagirdari**: revenue assignment instead of cash salary. Jagirdari crisis is a major cause of decline.
- Decline causes: weak successors, Aurangzeb's Deccan and religious policies, jagirdari crisis, Marathas, invasions of Nadir Shah (1739) and Abdali (3rd Panipat 1761), European companies.
## Marathas and Sikhs
- **Shivaji** (1627-80): guerrilla warfare, forts, coronation at Raigad 1674; **Ashtapradhan** council (Peshwa at head); taxes **chauth** (1/4) and **sardeshmukhi** (1/10). Later Peshwas (Baji Rao I) built a confederacy - Scindia, Holkar, Gaekwad, Bhonsle.
- **Sikhism**: Guru Nanak (b. 1469) -> Guru Angad (Gurmukhi) -> Guru Ram Das (Amritsar) -> Guru Arjan (Adi Granth, Harmandir Sahib) -> Guru Hargobind (miri-piri) -> Guru Gobind Singh (**Khalsa 1699**). Ranjit Singh built the Sikh empire (Treaty of Amritsar 1809).
## Bhakti and Sufi Movements
- **Bhakti**: devotion over ritual, equality, regional languages. South: Alvars (Vishnu), Nayanars (Shiva). Philosophers: Shankara (Advaita), Ramanuja (Vishishtadvaita), Madhva (Dvaita). North: **Nirguna** - Kabir, Nanak; **Saguna** - Tulsidas, Surdas, Mirabai, Chaitanya; Maharashtra - Jnaneshwar, Namdev, Tukaram; Assam - Sankardeva; Karnataka - Basavanna (Lingayats).
- **Sufism**: mystical Islam; pir-murid, khanqah, sama. Orders (silsilas): **Chishti** (Moinuddin Chishti at Ajmer, Nizamuddin Auliya - most popular, kept away from the state), Suhrawardi, Qadiri, Naqshbandi (orthodox).
- Impact: composite culture, growth of Hindi, Punjabi, Bengali, Marathi literature, social reform.

## Common Misconceptions
- "Aryans destroyed Harappa" - not supported; decline was gradual and environmental.
- "Ashoka became a monk and gave up the army" - he kept the army and warned forest tribes.
- "Akbar started a new religion" - Din-i-Ilahi was an ethical order with very few followers.
## Prelims MCQs with Explanations
Q: Which Harappan site has a dockyard? (a) Kalibangan (b) Lothal (c) Dholavira (d) Banawali
A: (b) Lothal in Gujarat. Kalibangan = ploughed field; Dholavira = reservoirs.
Q: The term "Dhamma Mahamatra" is associated with (a) Chandragupta (b) Ashoka (c) Kanishka (d) Harsha
A: (b) Ashoka appointed them to spread Dhamma (Rock Edict V).
Q: Uttaramerur inscription gives information about (a) Chola village administration (b) Pallava trade (c) Gupta coins (d) Pandya navy
A: (a). It describes the qualifications and election of ward members.
Q: "Zabt" system under Akbar relates to (a) army (b) land revenue (c) justice (d) trade
A: (b). Measurement of land and fixing cash revenue on 10-year average (Dahsala) by Todar Mal.
Q: Which Sufi order was the earliest and most popular in India? (a) Naqshbandi (b) Qadiri (c) Chishti (d) Suhrawardi
A: (c) Chishti.
Q: Consider: 1. Milinda Panha - Menander 2. Indica - Megasthenes 3. Harshacharita - Kalidasa. Which are correct?
A: 1 and 2 only. Harshacharita was written by Banabhatta.
## Mains Questions with Model Structures
Q: The Gupta period is called the Golden Age. Critically examine. (150 words)
Structure: Intro - define period. Body 1 - achievements: literature, science, art, coins. Body 2 - limits: feudal land grants, declining trade, caste rigidity, women's status. Conclusion - golden for elite culture, not for all sections.
Q: Bhakti and Sufi movements created a composite culture. Discuss.
Structure: Intro - both rose as devotional paths. Body - shared ideas (love of God, equality, guru/pir), regional languages, music (qawwali, kirtan), figures like Kabir and Nanak, shrines visited by all. Limits - did not end caste. Conclusion - foundation of India's syncretic tradition (link to Art. 51A(f)).
Q: Why did Magadha emerge as the first empire?
Structure: geography + resources + rulers + army + administration; conclude with the Mauryan state as the result.
## Revision Sheet
- IVC = urban, bronze, undeciphered script. Vedic = rural, iron later. 6th c. BCE = second urbanisation + new religions.
- Maurya = centralised; Gupta = decentralised; Chola = local self-government; Sultanate = iqta; Mughal = mansab + jagir.
- Battles: Tarain 1191/1192, Panipat 1526/1556/1761, Talikota 1565, Haldighati 1576.
