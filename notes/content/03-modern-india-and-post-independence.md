# History II - Modern India and Post-Independence
Modern History gives 12-18 Prelims questions and a full section in GS I. Learn it as one story: Europeans come to trade -> the Company conquers -> the economy is drained -> Indians resist -> a mass national movement wins freedom -> the new nation is built.
## Arrival of Europeans
| Power | Year / first base | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| Portuguese | 1498 Vasco da Gama at Calicut | Albuquerque took Goa 1510; Blue Water Policy (Almeida); left only in 1961 |
| Dutch | 1602 VOC; Masulipatnam | defeated at Colachel (1741) by Travancore |
| English | 1600 EIC; Surat 1613 | Madras 1639, Bombay 1668, Calcutta 1690 |
| Danes | 1616 | Tranquebar, Serampore |
| French | 1664; Pondicherry 1674 | Dupleix; lost at Wandiwash (1760) |
- **Carnatic Wars** (1746-63): English vs French. Third war ended French hopes (Treaty of Paris 1763).
- Why the English won: naval power, strong finance (Bank of England), private company with government backing, Bengal's revenue, better leadership.
## British Conquest of India
- **Plassey 1757**: Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daula through Mir Jafar's betrayal - gave a foothold.
- **Buxar 1764**: Munro defeated Mir Qasim + Shuja-ud-Daula + Shah Alam II - real foundation. **Treaty of Allahabad 1765**: Diwani (revenue rights) of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa; **Dual Government** till 1772.
- Mysore: four wars; Tipu Sultan died at Seringapatam 1799. Marathas: three wars, ended 1818. Sikhs: two wars, Punjab annexed 1849.
| Policy | Governor-General | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Ring Fence | Warren Hastings | buffer states to protect Bengal |
| Subsidiary Alliance | Wellesley (1798) | Indian ruler keeps British troops, pays for them, loses foreign policy; first Hyderabad |
| Doctrine of Lapse | Dalhousie (1848-56) | state without natural heir annexed: Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur; Awadh on "misgovernment" |

## Economic Impact of British Rule
| Land revenue system | Where / who | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Permanent Settlement 1793 | Cornwallis; Bengal, Bihar, Orissa | zamindar is owner; revenue fixed forever; sunset law |
| Ryotwari 1820 | Munro, Reed; Madras, Bombay | direct settlement with cultivator (ryot) |
| Mahalwari 1822/33 | Holt Mackenzie, Bentinck; NW Provinces, Punjab | village (mahal) jointly responsible |
- **Deindustrialisation**: one-way free trade ruined handloom weavers; India became exporter of raw cotton and importer of cloth.
- **Commercialisation of agriculture**: indigo, cotton, opium - food shortages and famines (Bengal 1770, 1943).
- **Drain of Wealth**: Dadabhai Naoroji in *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India* - Home Charges, salaries, profits flowed to Britain without return. R.C. Dutt and M.G. Ranade developed the economic critique.
- Railways (1853 Bombay-Thane), telegraph and post served British trade and control but also unified the market and the people.
## Resistance before 1857 and the Revolt of 1857
- Tribal and peasant revolts: Sanyasi-Fakir, Paika (1817), Kol, **Santhal hul (1855-56, Sidhu and Kanhu)**, Indigo revolt (1859-60, Dinabandhu Mitra's Neel Darpan), later **Munda Ulgulan (1899-1900, Birsa Munda)**.
- **1857 causes**: political (Lapse, Awadh), economic (ruin of peasants, artisans), social-religious (fear of conversion, reforms), military (low pay, General Service Enlistment Act), immediate (greased cartridges of Enfield rifle).
| Centre | Leader |
|---|---|
| Delhi | Bahadur Shah Zafar, Bakht Khan |
| Kanpur | Nana Saheb, Tantia Tope |
| Lucknow | Begum Hazrat Mahal |
| Jhansi | Rani Lakshmibai |
| Bihar (Jagdishpur) | Kunwar Singh |
| Faizabad | Maulvi Ahmadullah |
- Began at Meerut, 10 May 1857. Failed due to no unified leadership or plan, limited spread (south, Punjab, Bengal quiet), princes and educated class aloof, superior British resources.
- Results: Company rule ended (Act of 1858), Queen's Proclamation, army reorganised, divide and rule, end of annexations.
- Nature: "Sepoy Mutiny" (British view) vs "First War of Independence" (V.D. Savarkar). Balanced view: more than a mutiny, less than a national war.

## Indian National Movement - Three Phases
@chain Moderates 1885-1905 -> Extremists 1905-1919 -> Gandhian era 1919-1947 :: Phases of the freedom struggle
### Moderate phase (1885-1905)
- **INC founded 1885** at Bombay; A.O. Hume; first president W.C. Bonnerjee; 72 delegates. Leaders: Naoroji, Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta, S.N. Banerjea.
- Methods: prayer, petition, protest. Achievement: economic critique of colonialism, political education.
### Extremist phase and Swadeshi
- **Partition of Bengal 1905** (Curzon) -> **Swadeshi and Boycott**. Lal-Bal-Pal and Aurobindo. Tilak: "Swaraj is my birthright".
- 1906 Calcutta session: Swaraj as goal; **Muslim League** founded at Dacca 1906. **Surat Split 1907**. Partition annulled 1911; capital shifted to Delhi.
- **Ghadar Party** 1913 (San Francisco, Lala Hardayal); **Home Rule Leagues 1916** (Tilak, Besant); **Lucknow Pact 1916** (Congress-League unity, Congress accepted separate electorates).
- Montagu Declaration 1917: responsible government as aim.

| 1917 | Champaran | first civil disobedience; indigo tinkathia system |

| 1919 | Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh (13 April) | Hunter Commission; Tagore renounced knighthood |

- **Subhas Bose**: Congress president 1938, 1939 (Tripuri); Forward Bloc; INA at Singapore 1943; "Delhi Chalo"; Azad Hind Government. Captain Lakshmi Sahgal led the Rani of Jhansi Regiment.
- **Peasants and workers**: Bardoli (1928, Patel became "Sardar"), Moplah (1921), Eka, All India Kisan Sabha (1936, Sahajanand), Tebhaga (1946), Telangana (1946-51); AITUC (1920, Lala Lajpat Rai).
- **Women**: Sarojini Naidu (Dharasana), Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay, Aruna Asaf Ali (hoisted flag 1942), Usha Mehta (Congress Radio), Rani Gaidinliu (Naga), Matangini Hazra.
### Why Gandhi's methods worked
Analogy: Satyagraha is like a strike by the whole nation - the ruler cannot rule if the ruled refuse to cooperate.
- Mass base (peasants, women, workers), simple symbols (salt, khadi), moral pressure, phases of Struggle-Truce-Struggle that let people rest and regroup.
## Post-Independence India
### Integration of princely states
- 565 states. **Sardar Patel** and **V.P. Menon** used the **Instrument of Accession** (defence, external affairs, communications).
| State | Problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Junagadh | Muslim nawab acceded to Pakistan, Hindu majority | plebiscite, Feb 1948 |
| Hyderabad | Nizam wanted independence; Razakars | **Operation Polo**, Sept 1948 |
| Kashmir | Maharaja Hari Singh; tribal invasion Oct 1947 | signed accession 26 Oct 1947 |
| Goa | Portuguese | Operation Vijay 1961 |
| Sikkim | protectorate | 22nd state in 1975 (36th Amendment) |
### Reorganisation of states
- Dhar Commission and JVP Committee rejected language basis. Death of **Potti Sriramulu** -> **Andhra State 1953** (first linguistic state).
- **Fazl Ali Commission** (with Panikkar, Kunzru) -> **States Reorganisation Act 1956**: 14 states, 6 UTs.
- Later: Maharashtra-Gujarat 1960, Nagaland 1963, Punjab-Haryana 1966, Himachal 1971, NE states 1972, Goa 1987, Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand 2000, Telangana 2014. J&K reorganised into two UTs in 2019. Today: 28 states, 8 UTs.
### Nation building
- **Planning**: Planning Commission 1950; First Plan (Harrod-Domar, agriculture); Second Plan (Mahalanobis, heavy industry); NITI Aayog replaced it in 2015.
- **Land reforms**: abolition of zamindari, tenancy reform, ceilings, consolidation; Bhoodan (Vinoba Bhave 1951). Success limited except in Kerala, West Bengal (Operation Barga).
- **Green Revolution** (mid-1960s): HYV seeds, fertiliser, irrigation; M.S. Swaminathan, C. Subramaniam, Norman Borlaug; Punjab, Haryana, west UP. **White Revolution**: Operation Flood 1970, Verghese Kurien, Amul.
- **Foreign policy**: Panchsheel 1954, NAM 1961. Wars: 1947-48, 1962 (China), 1965, 1971 (Bangladesh; Shimla Agreement 1972), Kargil 1999.
- **Bank nationalisation**: 14 banks in 1969, 6 in 1980. Privy purses abolished 1971 (26th Amendment).
- **Emergency 1975-77**: after Allahabad HC verdict; Art. 352 on "internal disturbance"; 42nd Amendment; press censorship; Janata government 1977; 44th Amendment replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion".
- 1980s-90s: Punjab crisis, Assam Accord 1985, Mandal Commission implemented 1990 (Indra Sawhney 1992), Panchayati Raj 1992-93.
- **1991 reforms**: BoP crisis (forex for 2 weeks of imports) -> LPG under P.V. Narasimha Rao and Manmohan Singh: delicensing, devaluation, FDI, trade opening.

## Prelims MCQs with Explanations
Q: The Dual Government in Bengal was abolished by (a) Clive (b) Warren Hastings (c) Cornwallis (d) Wellesley
A: (b) in 1772.
Q: Separate electorates were first introduced by (a) 1892 (b) 1909 (c) 1919 (d) 1935 Act
A: (b) Morley-Minto reforms 1909.
Q: The Poona Pact dealt with (a) Hindu-Muslim unity (b) depressed classes' representation (c) princely states (d) labour
A: (b). Separate electorates under the Communal Award were replaced by reserved seats (147) in joint electorates.
Q: Which was withdrawn after Chauri Chaura? (a) Swadeshi (b) Non-Cooperation (c) Civil Disobedience (d) Quit India
A: (b).
Q: The Cabinet Mission Plan 1946 proposed (a) partition (b) three-tier union with grouping of provinces (c) dominion status in 1948 (d) dyarchy
A: (b).
Q: Arrange: 1. Lucknow Pact 2. Rowlatt Act 3. Surat Split 4. Dandi March
A: 3 (1907), 1 (1916), 2 (1919), 4 (1930).
## Mains Questions with Model Structures
Q: The 1857 revolt was the culmination of a century of discontent. Explain.
Structure: Intro - 100 years after Plassey. Body - political, economic, socio-religious, military causes with examples; earlier revolts. Conclusion - immediate cause only a spark; outcome changed the nature of British rule.
Q: Assess the role of women in the freedom struggle.
Structure: early reformers -> Swadeshi -> Gandhian mass participation (salt, picketing) -> revolutionaries (Pritilata Waddedar, Kalpana Dutt) -> INA -> Constituent Assembly's 15 women. Conclusion - laid base for equal franchise.
Q: How did Sardar Patel integrate the princely states? What lessons does it hold for administrators?
Structure: carrot (privy purse), stick (police action), people's movements; lessons - firmness with flexibility, negotiation, national interest.
Q: Why were the 1991 reforms needed? Were they successful?
Structure: crisis indicators -> measures (LPG) -> gains (growth, forex, services) -> gaps (jobless growth, inequality, agriculture).
