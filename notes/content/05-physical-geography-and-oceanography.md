# Geography I - Earth, Physical Geography and Oceanography
Geography answers three questions: **Where** is it? **Why** is it there? **So what** for people? Always study with an atlas open. This notebook builds the physical base; the next one covers India and the world.
## Earth in the Universe
- Universe began with the **Big Bang** (about 13.8 billion years ago). Solar system formed about 4.6 billion years ago (nebular hypothesis).
- Planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars (inner, rocky); Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune (outer, gas/ice giants). Venus is hottest and rotates east to west; Jupiter largest; Pluto is a dwarf planet (2006).
- Asteroid belt lies between Mars and Jupiter. Comets have tails pointing away from the Sun. Goldilocks zone = distance allowing liquid water.
## Rotation, Revolution, Latitude and Longitude
- **Rotation**: west to east, about 24 hours -> day and night, Coriolis force. **Revolution**: 365.25 days on an axis tilted **23.5 degrees** -> seasons.
| Date | Event | Sun overhead at |
|---|---|---|
| 21 March | Spring equinox | Equator |
| 21 June | Summer solstice (longest day in N. hemisphere) | Tropic of Cancer 23.5 N |
| 23 September | Autumn equinox | Equator |
| 22 December | Winter solstice | Tropic of Capricorn 23.5 S |
- **Latitude**: angular distance north/south of Equator (0 to 90). **Longitude**: east/west of Prime Meridian (Greenwich, 0 to 180).
- Earth turns 360 degrees in 24 h -> **15 degrees = 1 hour, 1 degree = 4 minutes**. East is ahead ("East gain add, West lose subtract").
- **IST** = 82.5 E (through Mirzapur) = GMT + 5:30. **International Date Line** about 180 degrees: crossing eastward (Asia to America) you repeat a day; westward you lose a day.
### Problems with answers
Example: It is 12 noon at Greenwich. What is the local time at 75 E?
Solution: 75 x 4 min = 300 min = 5 h ahead -> **5:00 pm**.
Example: A cricket match starts at 10:00 am IST. What time is it in New York (75 W, ignoring daylight saving)?
Solution: Difference in longitude = 82.5 + 75 = 157.5 degrees -> 157.5 x 4 = 630 min = 10 h 30 min behind -> **11:30 pm of the previous day**.
- **Eclipses**: Solar - Moon between Sun and Earth (new moon). Lunar - Earth between Sun and Moon (full moon).
## Interior of the Earth
@layers Crust: 5-70 km, SIAL over SIMA | Mantle: to 2900 km, asthenosphere is semi-molten | Outer core: liquid iron-nickel, creates magnetism | Inner core: solid, about 5500 C :: Layers of the Earth
- Evidence: seismic waves. **P-waves** (fast, travel through solid and liquid), **S-waves** (only solids -> shadow zone proves liquid outer core), surface waves (L, Rayleigh - most destructive).
- Discontinuities: Conrad, **Mohorovicic** (crust-mantle), **Gutenberg** (mantle-core), Lehmann (outer-inner core).
- Most abundant element in crust: oxygen, then silicon, aluminium. In whole Earth: iron.
## Rocks and the Rock Cycle
| Type | Formation | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Igneous | cooling of magma; intrusive (large crystals) or extrusive | granite, basalt |
| Sedimentary | deposition in layers; contain fossils, coal, petroleum | sandstone, limestone, shale |
| Metamorphic | change by heat and pressure | marble (from limestone), slate (shale), quartzite (sandstone), gneiss (granite) |
@chain Magma -> Igneous -> weathering -> Sedimentary -> heat and pressure -> Metamorphic -> melting -> Magma :: The rock cycle
## Continental Drift, Sea-floor Spreading and Plate Tectonics
- **Wegener (1912)**: Pangaea broke into Laurasia and Gondwana; evidence - jigsaw fit, fossils (Mesosaurus, Glossopteris), matching rocks, tillite. He could not explain the force.
- **Sea-floor spreading** (Hess): new crust forms at mid-ocean ridges; magnetic stripes prove it.
- **Plate tectonics**: lithosphere is broken into 7 major plates moving on the asthenosphere by convection currents.
| Boundary | Motion | Result | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Divergent | apart | ridges, rift valleys | Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East African Rift |
| Convergent | together | trenches, fold mountains, volcanoes | Himalayas (C-C), Andes (O-C), Mariana Trench (O-O) |
| Transform | slide past | earthquakes | San Andreas Fault |
- The Indian plate moved north from Gondwana, closed the Tethys Sea and collided with the Eurasian plate -> **Himalayas** (still rising; hence earthquakes in north India).
## Earthquakes and Volcanoes
- **Focus** = point of origin; **epicentre** = point above on the surface. Magnitude: Richter / moment magnitude scale (energy, logarithmic); intensity: Mercalli scale (damage).
- India has four seismic zones (II to V). Zone V: NE India, Kashmir, Uttarakhand, Kutch, Andamans.
- **Volcanoes**: shield (basaltic, gentle - Hawaii), composite/strato (explosive - Fujiyama, Vesuvius), calderas. 75% lie on the **Pacific Ring of Fire**. India: **Barren Island** (only active), Narcondam (dormant). Deccan Traps = flood basalts -> black soil.
- Tsunami: caused by undersea earthquakes; 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami led to the Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre at INCOIS, Hyderabad.
## Landforms
- **Endogenic** forces build (folding, faulting, volcanism); **exogenic** forces wear down (weathering, erosion, deposition).
- Mountains: fold (Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies), block (Vosges, Satpura; with rift valleys - Narmada, Tapi), volcanic, residual (Aravalli - one of the oldest fold mountains, now residual).
| Agent | Erosional forms | Depositional forms |
|---|---|---|
| River | V-valley, gorge, canyon, waterfall, meander | alluvial fan, floodplain, levee, ox-bow lake, delta |
| Glacier | U-valley, cirque, arete, horn, hanging valley, fjord | moraines, drumlins, eskers |
| Wind | mushroom rock, yardang, inselberg | sand dunes (barchan), loess |
| Sea waves | cliff, cave, arch, stack | beach, spit, bar, lagoon, tombolo |
| Groundwater (karst) | sinkhole, cave | stalactite (hangs), stalagmite (rises) |
Tip: Stalactite holds "tight" to the ceiling; stalagmite "might" reach it from the ground.
## Atmosphere
- Composition: Nitrogen 78%, Oxygen 21%, Argon 0.93%, CO2 about 0.04%.
@layers Exosphere | Thermosphere: ionosphere reflects radio waves, auroras | Mesosphere: coldest, meteors burn | Stratosphere: ozone layer, jets fly here | Troposphere: weather, 8 km at poles to 18 km at equator :: Layers of the atmosphere (top to bottom)
- Temperature falls with height in the troposphere at about **6.5 C per km** (normal lapse rate). **Temperature inversion** (cold air trapped below warm air) causes fog and smog, as in Delhi winters.
- **Insolation** = incoming solar radiation. **Albedo** = reflectivity (snow high, forest low). Earth's heat budget balances incoming short-wave and outgoing long-wave radiation; greenhouse gases trap the outgoing part.
### Pressure belts and winds
@layers Polar high 90 | Sub-polar low 60 | Sub-tropical high 30 (horse latitudes) | Equatorial low 0 (doldrums) :: Pressure belts in each hemisphere
- **Planetary winds**: Trade winds (30 to 0, north-east and south-east), Westerlies (30 to 60; Roaring Forties in the south), Polar easterlies.
- **Coriolis force** deflects winds to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern (Ferrel's law); zero at the equator.
- Cells: Hadley (0-30), Ferrel (30-60), Polar. **ITCZ** = belt where trade winds meet; it shifts north in summer and pulls the monsoon over India.
- Local winds: Loo (hot, north India), Chinook and Foehn (warm, dry, leeward), Mistral (cold), Sirocco (Sahara), Kalbaisakhi (Bengal thunderstorms), Mango showers.
- **Jet streams**: fast upper-air westerly winds; sub-tropical westerly jet brings western disturbances in winter; tropical easterly jet helps the monsoon.
### Moisture, rainfall and cyclones
- Humidity: absolute, specific, relative. Dew point = temperature of saturation.
- Clouds: cirrus (high, feathery), cumulus (cotton), stratus (sheet), **cumulonimbus** (thunderstorm), nimbostratus (steady rain).
| Rainfall type | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Convectional | heated air rises | equatorial afternoons |
| Orographic | air forced up a mountain; leeward side is rain shadow | Western Ghats: Mumbai wet, Pune dry |
| Cyclonic / frontal | warm and cold air masses meet | temperate regions |
| Feature | Tropical cyclone | Temperate cyclone |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | warm seas above 27 C, 5-20 degrees latitude | fronts, 35-65 degrees |
| Energy | latent heat of condensation | temperature contrast |
| Movement | east to west | west to east |
| Structure | calm **eye**, eyewall | fronts, no eye |
| Names | cyclone (Indian Ocean), hurricane (Atlantic), typhoon (W. Pacific), willy-willy (Australia) | - |
- Bay of Bengal has more cyclones than the Arabian Sea: warmer water, lower salinity from rivers, remnants of Pacific typhoons. Cyclones do not form at the equator (no Coriolis force).
- Anticyclone = high-pressure centre, clear weather, clockwise in the north.

## Oceanography
- Oceans cover 71% of Earth. Pacific (largest, deepest - Challenger Deep in Mariana Trench, about 11 km), Atlantic (S-shaped, busiest), Indian, Southern, Arctic.
@chain Continental shelf -> Continental slope -> Continental rise -> Abyssal plain -> Mid-ocean ridge / Trench :: Ocean floor relief
- Continental shelf: shallow, rich in fish, oil and gas (Mumbai High).
- **Temperature** falls with depth; thermocline = zone of rapid fall. **Salinity** average 35 parts per thousand; highest in sub-tropics and enclosed seas (Dead Sea, Red Sea); low near the equator and river mouths (Bay of Bengal less salty than Arabian Sea).
### Ocean currents
- Caused by planetary winds, Coriolis force, temperature and salinity differences. Gyres circulate clockwise in the north, anticlockwise in the south.
| Warm currents | Cold currents |
|---|---|
| Gulf Stream, North Atlantic Drift | Labrador |
| Kuroshio | Oyashio |
| Brazil | Benguela, Canary |
| Agulhas, East Australian | Humboldt (Peru), California |
- Warm currents flow on the east coasts in low latitudes; cold currents on the west coasts -> **deserts on the western margins** (Atacama, Namib, Kalahari).
- Meeting of warm and cold currents = best fishing grounds: Grand Banks (Gulf Stream + Labrador), Japan (Kuroshio + Oyashio).
- In the North Indian Ocean, currents **reverse with the monsoon** - unique.
### Tides
- Caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon (mainly) and the Sun. **Spring tide**: Sun, Earth, Moon in a line (new and full moon) - highest. **Neap tide**: at right angles (quarter moons) - lowest. Two high tides in about 24 h 50 min. Highest tides: Bay of Fundy; in India, Gulf of Khambhat and Kutch (tidal energy potential).
### El Nino, La Nina and the Indian Ocean Dipole
- Normal year: trade winds push warm water to the west Pacific; cold upwelling off Peru; Walker circulation.
- **El Nino**: trade winds weaken, warm water spreads to the central and east Pacific -> drought risk in India, Australia, Indonesia; floods in Peru. **La Nina**: opposite, usually good monsoon. Together = **ENSO**.
- **IOD**: positive IOD (warm western Indian Ocean) helps the Indian monsoon and can offset El Nino (as in 1997, 2019).
Trap: El Nino raises the probability of a weak monsoon; it is not a guarantee.
### Coral reefs and marine resources
- Corals are animals (polyps) living with algae (zooxanthellae). Need warm (23-25 C), shallow, clear, saline water. Types: fringing, barrier (Great Barrier Reef), atoll (Lakshadweep). **Bleaching** = corals expel algae when water warms.
- India's reefs: Andaman and Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Gulf of Mannar, Gulf of Kutch.
- UNCLOS zones: territorial sea 12 nautical miles, contiguous zone 24, **EEZ 200**. Resources: fish, polymetallic nodules (India's Deep Ocean Mission, Samudrayaan), gas hydrates.
## Climate Change in One Page
- Greenhouse gases: CO2, methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour, fluorinated gases. Evidence: 1.1-1.3 C warming since pre-industrial times, glacier retreat, sea-level rise, more extreme events. Details in the Environment notebook.
## Prelims MCQs with Explanations
Q: Which seismic waves cannot pass through liquids? (a) P (b) S (c) L (d) all pass
A: (b) S-waves - this proves the outer core is liquid.
Q: Deserts on the western margins of continents are largely due to (a) warm currents (b) cold currents and offshore trade winds (c) westerlies (d) mountains
A: (b).
Q: Consider: 1. Coriolis force is maximum at the equator. 2. Jet streams occur in the upper troposphere. Correct?
A: 2 only. Coriolis force is zero at the equator and maximum at the poles.
Q: Spring tides occur on (a) quarter moon days (b) new and full moon days (c) only eclipses (d) solstices
A: (b).
Q: During El Nino, sea surface temperature rises in (a) western Pacific (b) central and eastern Pacific (c) Arabian Sea (d) north Atlantic
A: (b).
Q: The Himalayas were formed at which boundary? (a) divergent (b) continent-continent convergent (c) transform (d) ocean-ocean convergent
A: (b).
