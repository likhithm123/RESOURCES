## India - Location and Extent
- Latitudes **8 4' N to 37 6' N**; longitudes **68 7' E to 97 25' E**. Tropic of Cancer (23.5 N) crosses 8 states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram.
- Area 3.28 million sq km (7th largest); land frontier about 15,200 km; coastline about 7,500 km (recalculated to over 11,000 km with new measurement methods). North-south 3,214 km; east-west 2,933 km.
- Southernmost point: **Indira Point** (Nicobar); mainland: Kanyakumari. Time difference between Gujarat and Arunachal: about 2 hours.
| Neighbour | Border length rank / note |
|---|---|
| Bangladesh | longest (about 4,096 km); 5 Indian states |
| China | LAC; McMahon Line in the east |
| Pakistan | Radcliffe Line; LoC in J&K |
| Nepal | open border; 5 states (Uttarakhand, UP, Bihar, WB, Sikkim) |
| Myanmar | 4 NE states (Arunachal, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram) |
| Bhutan | 4 states (Sikkim, WB, Assam, Arunachal) |
| Afghanistan | shortest (Durand Line, via PoK) |
- Sea neighbours: Sri Lanka (Palk Strait), Maldives (Eight Degree Channel separates Minicoy from Maldives). Ten Degree Channel separates Andaman from Nicobar; Nine Degree Channel separates Minicoy from the rest of Lakshadweep.
## Physiographic Divisions
@layers Himalayas | Northern Plains | Peninsular Plateau | Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, Islands :: Six physical divisions of India
### The Himalayas
- Young fold mountains, about 2,400 km, three parallel ranges:
| Range | Other name | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Greater Himalaya | Himadri | average 6,000 m; Everest (Nepal), Kanchenjunga (India's highest peak inside undisputed territory), Nanda Devi |
| Lesser Himalaya | Himachal | hill stations (Shimla, Mussoorie), Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar |
| Outer Himalaya | Shiwaliks | youngest; **duns** (Dehradun) |
- Trans-Himalaya: Karakoram (K2/Godwin Austen), Ladakh, Zaskar. Purvanchal hills: Patkai, Naga, Mizo.
- Passes: Zoji La (Srinagar-Leh), Rohtang, Shipki La (Himachal-Tibet), Lipulekh (Uttarakhand), Nathu La and Jelep La (Sikkim), Bomdi La (Arunachal).
- Significance: climate barrier, source of perennial rivers, forests, hydropower, defence, tourism.
### Northern Plains
- Formed by alluvium of Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra. Belts from the foothills: **Bhabar** (pebbles, streams disappear) -> **Terai** (marshy, re-emerge) -> **Bhangar** (old alluvium, kankar) -> **Khadar** (new fertile alluvium).
### Peninsular Plateau
- Oldest landmass (Gondwana). **Central Highlands** (Malwa, Bundelkhand, Chotanagpur - mineral storehouse) and **Deccan Plateau** (basalt, black soil).
| Feature | Western Ghats (Sahyadri) | Eastern Ghats |
|---|---|---|
| Continuity | continuous, crossed by passes (Thal, Bhor, Pal ghat) | broken by rivers |
| Height | higher; Anamudi 2,695 m (highest in south India) | lower; Jindhagada/Mahendragiri |
| Rainfall | heavy orographic | moderate |
| Status | UNESCO site, biodiversity hotspot | - |
- Hills meet at the **Nilgiris**. Aravallis: Guru Shikhar (Mount Abu). Vindhya and Satpura flank the Narmada rift.
### Coasts and islands
- West coast: narrow, estuaries; Konkan, Kanara, Malabar (backwaters/kayals). East coast: wide, deltas; Northern Circars, Coromandel (winter rain).
- **Andaman and Nicobar**: volcanic origin, extension of Arakan Yoma; Saddle Peak. **Lakshadweep**: coral atolls.
## Drainage System
| Feature | Himalayan rivers | Peninsular rivers |
|---|---|---|
| Flow | perennial (snow + rain) | seasonal (rain) |
| Course | long, meanders, shifting | fixed, shorter |
| Valleys | deep gorges, V-shaped | shallow, graded |
| Age | young, antecedent | old |
- **Indus**: rises near Mansarovar; tributaries Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej. Indus Waters Treaty 1960: eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India, western to Pakistan (treaty put in abeyance by India in 2025).
- **Ganga**: Bhagirathi (Gangotri) + Alaknanda meet at **Devprayag**. Left bank: Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi ("sorrow of Bihar"). Right bank: **Yamuna** (with Chambal, Betwa, Ken), Son.
- **Brahmaputra**: Tsangpo in Tibet -> Siang/Dihang in Arunachal -> Jamuna in Bangladesh; Majuli river island.
- **East-flowing peninsular** (deltas): Mahanadi, **Godavari** (longest peninsular, Dakshin Ganga), Krishna, Kaveri. **West-flowing** (estuaries, through rift valleys): **Narmada, Tapi**; also Mahi, Sabarmati, Luni (inland).
Mnemonic: "Panch Prayag" on the Alaknanda downstream: Vishnu, Nand, Karn, Rudra, Dev.
- Projects: Bhakra Nangal (Sutlej), Hirakud (Mahanadi), Tehri (Bhagirathi), Sardar Sarovar (Narmada), Nagarjuna Sagar (Krishna), Polavaram (Godavari). Lakes: Wular (largest freshwater, tectonic), Chilika (largest brackish lagoon), Sambhar (salt), Loktak (floating phumdis), Vembanad.
## Climate and the Indian Monsoon
- Monsoon = seasonal reversal of winds. Mechanism: differential heating of land and sea; northward shift of **ITCZ**; high pressure near Madagascar (Mascarene High); Tibetan plateau heating; tropical easterly jet; withdrawal of the sub-tropical westerly jet; ENSO and IOD.
- Onset: Kerala about **1 June**; covers India by mid-July. Two branches: Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal.
| Season | Months | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Winter | Dec-Feb | western disturbances bring rain to NW India (good for wheat) |
| Summer | Mar-May | loo, mango showers, kalbaisakhi |
| SW monsoon | Jun-Sep | about 75% of annual rain |
| Retreating monsoon | Oct-Nov | "October heat"; NE monsoon rain to **Tamil Nadu coast** |
- Wettest: Mawsynram/Cherrapunji (funnel-shaped Khasi hills). Rain shadow: leeward Western Ghats. Thar is dry because the Aravallis lie parallel to the winds.

| Alluvial | northern plains, deltas | most widespread, fertile; rich in potash, poor in nitrogen |
| Black (regur) | Deccan trap | holds moisture, self-ploughing; cotton |

| Laterite | high rain and heat; W. Ghats, NE | leached; tea, coffee, cashew |
| Arid | Rajasthan | saline, kankar |
| Forest / mountain | Himalaya | humus |
| Forest type | Rainfall | Trees |
|---|---|---|
| Tropical evergreen | above 200 cm | rosewood, ebony, mahogany |
| Tropical deciduous (monsoon) | 70-200 cm | teak, sal - most widespread |
| Thorn | below 70 cm | babool, cactus |

- India State of Forest Report: forest and tree cover is about 25% of area; Madhya Pradesh has the largest forest area; target of the National Forest Policy 1988 is 33%.
## Agriculture
- Seasons: **Kharif** (Jun-Oct: rice, maize, cotton, soybean, groundnut), **Rabi** (Oct-Mar: wheat, gram, mustard, barley), **Zaid** (summer: watermelon, vegetables).
| Crop | Conditions | Leading states |
|---|---|---|
| Rice | 25 C+, 100 cm+ | West Bengal, UP, Punjab, Telangana |
| Wheat | cool growing, 50-75 cm | UP, MP, Punjab, Haryana |
| Cotton | black soil, 210 frost-free days | Gujarat, Maharashtra, Telangana |
| Sugarcane | hot, humid, 75-100 cm | UP, Maharashtra, Karnataka |
| Tea | slopes, acidic soil, 150 cm+ | Assam, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu |
| Coffee | shade, hills | Karnataka, Kerala |
| Jute | alluvial, hot humid | West Bengal |
- Irrigation: wells and tubewells largest source; canals in north; tanks in south. Net sown area about 140 million hectares.

Iron and steel near coal-iron belts: Jamshedpur (TISCO 1907), Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur, Bokaro. Cotton textile: Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Coimbatore. IT: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune.
## Population, Migration, Urbanisation and Transport
- Census 2011: 121 crore; density 382 per sq km; sex ratio 943; literacy 74% (male 82, female 65.5); urban 31.2%. Highest density Bihar; lowest Arunachal. Most populous UP. India became the world's most populous country in 2023. The next Census with caste enumeration is scheduled with reference date 1 March 2027.
- **Demographic transition**: Stage 1 high birth and death -> Stage 2 falling deaths, population explosion -> Stage 3 falling births -> Stage 4 low both. India is in late Stage 3; TFR about 2.0 (below replacement 2.1). **Demographic dividend** window roughly till 2040s.
- Migration: push (poverty, drought) and pull (jobs, education) factors; mostly rural to rural for marriage among women, rural to urban for work among men.
- Transport: Golden Quadrilateral, North-South and East-West corridors, Bharatmala, Sagarmala, Dedicated Freight Corridors. 13 major ports (12 government + Kamarajar corporate): Kandla/Deendayal, Mumbai, JNPT (largest container), Mormugao, New Mangalore, Kochi, Tuticorin, Chennai, Ennore, Visakhapatnam (deepest landlocked), Paradip, Kolkata-Haldia (riverine), Vadhavan (new). National Waterway 1: Prayagraj-Haldia.

## Human and Economic Geography Concepts
- Sectors: primary (extraction), secondary (manufacturing), tertiary (services), quaternary (knowledge), quinary (decision-makers).
- Settlements: clustered, dispersed, linear. Urban problems: slums, congestion, pollution, urban heat island.
- Regional inequality: BIMARU lag vs southern and western states; aspirational districts programme.

## Prelims MCQs with Explanations
Q: Which river flows through a rift valley? (a) Godavari (b) Narmada (c) Krishna (d) Mahanadi
A: (b). Narmada and Tapi flow west through rift valleys and form estuaries.
Q: The Ten Degree Channel separates (a) Andaman and Nicobar (b) Lakshadweep and Maldives (c) India and Sri Lanka (d) Minicoy and Lakshadweep
A: (a).
Q: Black soil is formed from (a) granite (b) basalt (c) limestone (d) alluvium
A: (b) weathering of Deccan lava.
Q: Which state does not share a border with Myanmar? (a) Manipur (b) Mizoram (c) Tripura (d) Nagaland
A: (c) Tripura borders only Bangladesh.
Q: Winter rainfall in Tamil Nadu is due to (a) western disturbances (b) north-east monsoon (c) SW monsoon (d) cyclones only
A: (b).
Q: Strait of Hormuz connects (a) Red Sea and Gulf of Aden (b) Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman (c) Black Sea and Marmara (d) Java and Sumatra
A: (b).
## Mains Questions with Model Structures

Structure: raw-material orientation (Chotanagpur) -> port-based plants (Vizag) -> market and scrap-based mini plants -> policy. Add a map.
Q: Why is the Indian monsoon difficult to predict?
Structure: many interacting drivers (ENSO, IOD, MJO, jet streams, Tibetan plateau), climate change, data gaps -> IMD's improvements (Mission Mausam).
Q: Discuss the causes and consequences of internal migration in India.
Structure: push-pull, streams, effects on source (remittances, feminisation of agriculture) and destination (slums, labour supply), policy (One Nation One Ration Card, e-Shram).
