# Polity I - The Constitution, Rights and Duties
Polity is the highest-scoring subject of UPSC: 15-20 Prelims questions every year and almost the whole of GS II. It is also the easiest to master because it is logical. This notebook assumes you know nothing. Read slowly; every later topic (Parliament, courts, federalism) stands on these basics.
## First Principles - What is a State, a Government and a Constitution
- Imagine 140 crore people living together. They need rules (who makes them?), someone to carry out the rules (who?), and someone to settle fights about the rules (who?). These three jobs are the three **organs of government**.
| Organ | Job | In India (Union level) |
|---|---|---|
| Legislature | makes laws | Parliament |
| Executive | implements laws, runs administration | President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers, civil servants |
| Judiciary | interprets laws, settles disputes | Supreme Court, High Courts, lower courts |
- **State** (in political science) = population + territory + government + sovereignty. India is a State. Andhra Pradesh is a "state" only in the everyday sense of a province.
- **Government** is the agency through which the State acts. Governments change after elections; the State continues.
- **Constitution** = the supreme rule book. It says how the government is formed, what powers it has, what it cannot do, and what rights people have. Any ordinary law that violates it can be struck down.
Analogy: If the country is a cricket match, the Constitution is the rule book, Parliament can add playing conditions, the government is the team batting right now, and the Supreme Court is the third umpire.
- **Constitutionalism** = the idea of **limited government**. Having a constitution is not enough; power must actually be restrained by law.
- **Rule of law** (A.V. Dicey): no one is above law; equality before law; rights enforced by ordinary courts. In India it is part of the basic structure.
### Forms of government - learn these pairs
| Pair | Type A | Type B | India |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who is head of state | Monarchy (hereditary - UK, Japan) | Republic (elected - India, USA) | Republic |
| Executive-legislature relation | Parliamentary (executive is part of and responsible to legislature - UK) | Presidential (separate, fixed term - USA) | Parliamentary |
| Division of power by territory | Unitary (all power with centre - UK, France) | Federal (power divided by constitution - USA) | Federal with strong centre ("quasi-federal") |
| People's role | Direct democracy (referendum, initiative - Switzerland) | Indirect / representative | Representative |
## Making of the Constitution
Explanation: India did not write its Constitution in one day. For 90 years the British passed Acts that slowly introduced councils, elections and provinces. Our Constitution kept the useful machinery (about two-thirds of the 1935 Act) and added rights, democracy and social justice.
- Idea of a Constituent Assembly: first by **M.N. Roy (1934)**; Congress demanded it in 1935; accepted in the August Offer 1940; set up under the **Cabinet Mission Plan 1946**.
- Composition: 389 members (296 British India + 93 princely states); after partition 299. Members were **indirectly elected** by provincial assemblies - not by adult franchise.
| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 9 Dec 1946 | first meeting; Sachchidananda Sinha temporary president |
| 11 Dec 1946 | Dr Rajendra Prasad elected President; H.C. Mookerjee Vice-President; B.N. Rau constitutional adviser |
| 13 Dec 1946 | Nehru moved the **Objectives Resolution** (became the Preamble) |
| 22 July 1947 | national flag adopted |
| 29 Aug 1947 | Drafting Committee set up - chairman **Dr B.R. Ambedkar** |
| 26 Nov 1949 | Constitution adopted (Constitution Day); some provisions (citizenship, elections) came into force |
| 24 Jan 1950 | last sitting; national anthem and song adopted; Prasad elected first President |
| 26 Jan 1950 | Constitution commenced (date chosen for Purna Swaraj Day 1930) |
- Time taken: 2 years 11 months 18 days; 11 sessions. Original: Preamble, 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules. Today: about 470 Articles, 25 Parts, **12 Schedules**.
- Drafting Committee (7): Ambedkar, N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, K.M. Munshi, Mohammad Saadulla, N. Madhava Rau (replaced B.L. Mitter), T.T. Krishnamachari (replaced D.P. Khaitan).

## Salient Features
1. **Lengthiest written constitution** - because it covers centre and states both, a huge diverse country, and administrative detail.
2. **Blend of rigidity and flexibility** - some parts amended by simple majority, most by special majority, federal parts need states' consent too.
3. **Federal system with unitary bias** - "Union of States" (Art. 1): states cannot secede.
4. **Parliamentary form** - real executive (PM and Cabinet) is responsible to Lok Sabha.
5. **Synthesis of parliamentary sovereignty (UK) and judicial supremacy (USA)**.
6. **Integrated and independent judiciary** - one hierarchy enforces both central and state laws.
7. Fundamental Rights, DPSP, Fundamental Duties.
8. **Secular state**, **universal adult franchise** (voting age 21 lowered to 18 by the 61st Amendment, 1988), **single citizenship**.
9. Independent bodies (EC, CAG, UPSC), emergency provisions, three-tier government (73rd, 74th Amendments).

## The Preamble
Explanation: The Preamble is the introduction and the soul. It tells who made the Constitution (We, the people), what kind of country India is, and what goals it promises.
@flow (WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA - source of authority) | [Nature: Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic] | [Objectives: Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity] | (Adopted 26 November 1949) :: Structure of the Preamble
- **Sovereign**: India is not subject to any outside power. **Socialist** (added 1976): democratic socialism - reduce inequality, mixed economy. **Secular** (added 1976): state has no religion; equal respect to all (positive secularism). **Democratic**: government by elected representatives. **Republic**: head of state is elected.
- **Justice**: social, economic, political. **Liberty**: thought, expression, belief, faith, worship. **Equality**: status and opportunity. **Fraternity**: dignity of the individual and unity and integrity of the nation ("integrity" added 1976).
- Amended only once: **42nd Amendment 1976** added Socialist, Secular, Integrity.
| Case | Ruling on Preamble |
|---|---|
| Berubari Union (1960) | not a part of the Constitution |
| **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** | it **is** a part; can be amended without damaging basic structure |
| LIC of India (1995) | integral part, but not directly enforceable in court |
- It is neither a source of power nor a limit on power; it is a key to interpret unclear provisions.

## Union and its Territory (Articles 1-4)
- Art. 1: "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States." Union, not federation, because (a) it is not the result of an agreement among states and (b) no state can secede. India is "an indestructible Union of destructible states".
- Art. 2: admit or establish **new** states (e.g. Sikkim). Art. 3: form new states, change area, boundaries, names of **existing** states.
- Procedure under Art. 3: bill only on President's recommendation; President refers it to the state legislature for its **views** within a time limit; Parliament is **not bound** by those views; passes by **simple majority**. Art. 4: such laws are not amendments under Art. 368.
Example: Telangana (2014) was created although the Andhra Pradesh Assembly rejected the bill - Parliament's power is supreme here.
- Ceding Indian territory to a foreign country needs a constitutional amendment (Berubari case): 9th Amendment (1960), 100th Amendment (2015, land boundary with Bangladesh).
## Citizenship (Articles 5-11)
- The Constitution only says who was a citizen on 26 Jan 1950; it lets Parliament make the law - **Citizenship Act 1955**.
| Acquisition (5 ways) | Loss (3 ways) |
|---|---|
| birth | renunciation (voluntary) |
| descent | termination (acquiring another citizenship) |
| registration | deprivation (by government for fraud, disloyalty) |
| naturalisation | |
| incorporation of territory | |
- **Single citizenship**; no dual citizenship. **OCI** (Overseas Citizen of India) card gives lifelong visa and economic rights but no vote, no public office, no agricultural land.
- **Citizenship (Amendment) Act 2019**: fast-track citizenship for Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis, Christians from Pakistan, Bangladesh, Afghanistan who entered by 31 Dec 2014; rules notified 2024. Debate: Art. 14 and secularism vs. protection of persecuted minorities.
- Rights only for citizens: Arts 15, 16, 19, 29, 30; voting; holding offices like President, judge.
## Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12-35)
Explanation: Fundamental Rights (FRs) are the promises the State makes to every individual: "we will not treat you unequally, jail you without law, or stop your speech." If the State breaks a promise, you can go **directly to the Supreme Court**. That enforceability is what makes them "fundamental". Called the **Magna Carta of India**.
- Art. 12 defines "State": Parliament, governments, legislatures, local bodies, and other authorities (LIC, ONGC etc.). FRs are mainly against the State.
- Art. 13: laws inconsistent with FRs are void -> the basis of **judicial review**.
- FRs are **not absolute**; "reasonable restrictions" apply. Originally 7; Right to Property (Art. 31) removed by the 44th Amendment 1978 - now a legal right under **Art. 300A**.
| Right | Articles | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Equality | 14-18 | equality before law, no discrimination, equal opportunity, abolition of untouchability and titles |
| Freedom | 19-22 | six freedoms, protection in conviction, life and liberty, education, protection against arrest |
| Against exploitation | 23-24 | no trafficking, forced labour, child labour in hazardous work |
| Freedom of religion | 25-28 | conscience, manage religious affairs, no tax for religion, no religious instruction in state schools |
| Cultural and educational | 29-30 | protect language, script, culture; minorities may run institutions |
| Constitutional remedies | 32 | move the Supreme Court; writs |
### Right to Equality in detail
- **Art. 14**: "equality before law" (British, negative - no special privilege) + "equal protection of laws" (American, positive - equals treated equally). It forbids class legislation but allows **reasonable classification** (test: intelligible differentia + rational link to the object). It also forbids arbitrariness (E.P. Royappa, Maneka Gandhi).
Example: Giving a tax rebate to senior citizens is valid classification. Giving it only to people named "Ram" is arbitrary.
- **Art. 15**: no discrimination on grounds **only** of religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth. Exceptions: special provisions for women and children 15(3); for SEBCs, SCs, STs 15(4); in educational institutions including private ones 15(5); **EWS 10%** 15(6) by the 103rd Amendment 2019 (upheld in Janhit Abhiyan 2022).
- **Art. 16**: equality in public employment. Reservation for backward classes 16(4); promotion for SC/ST 16(4A); EWS 16(6). **Indra Sawhney (1992)**: 27% OBC upheld, creamy layer excluded, **50% ceiling**, no reservation in promotions (later reversed for SC/ST by the 77th Amendment).
- **Art. 17**: abolition of untouchability - an absolute right, also against private persons (Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955; SC/ST Atrocities Act 1989).
- **Art. 18**: abolition of titles; Bharat Ratna and Padma awards are not titles (Balaji Raghavan 1996).
### Right to Freedom in detail
- **Art. 19** (citizens only) - six freedoms and their restrictions:
| Clause | Freedom | Typical grounds of restriction |
|---|---|---|
| 19(1)(a) | speech and expression (includes press, silence, information, internet access as medium) | sovereignty, security, friendly relations, public order, decency, contempt, defamation, incitement |
| (b) | assemble peacefully without arms | public order |
| (c) | form associations, unions, cooperatives | public order, morality |
| (d) | move freely | general public interest, protect STs |
| (e) | reside and settle | same |
| (g) | profession, trade | qualifications, state monopoly |
- **Art. 20** (all persons): no ex-post-facto criminal law, no double jeopardy, no self-incrimination. **Art. 20 and 21 cannot be suspended even during Emergency** (44th Amendment).
- **Art. 21**: "No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law."
- A.K. Gopalan (1950): narrow view - any procedure made by law is enough. **Maneka Gandhi (1978)**: procedure must be **just, fair and reasonable** - effectively "due process". Since then Art. 21 has grown into a bundle of rights:
| Right read into Art. 21 | Case |
|---|---|
| livelihood | Olga Tellis (1985) |
| clean environment | M.C. Mehta, Subhash Kumar |
| privacy | K.S. Puttaswamy (2017, 9 judges) |
| dignity, health, shelter, speedy trial, legal aid | Francis Coralie, Hussainara Khatoon |
| against sexual harassment at work | Vishaka (1997) |
| passive euthanasia, living will | Common Cause (2018) |
| be free from adverse effects of climate change | M.K. Ranjitsinh (2024) |
- **Art. 21A**: free and compulsory education for children 6-14 (86th Amendment 2002; RTE Act 2009).
- **Art. 22**: rights of an arrested person - be told the grounds, consult a lawyer, produced before a magistrate within 24 hours. **Preventive detention**: up to 3 months without an Advisory Board's approval.
### Other rights
- Art. 23: bans trafficking, begar. Art. 24: no child below 14 in factories, mines, hazardous work.
- **Art. 25**: freedom of conscience and to profess, practise, propagate religion - subject to public order, morality, health; the State can regulate secular activities and make social reform. Art. 26: religious denominations manage their affairs. The court applies the "essential religious practices" test (Shirur Mutt; Sabarimala 2018; triple talaq struck down in Shayara Bano 2017).
- Art. 29: any section of citizens may conserve its language, script, culture. **Art. 30**: religious and linguistic minorities can establish and administer educational institutions.
- **Art. 32** - "heart and soul of the Constitution" (Ambedkar). Five writs:
| Writ | Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Habeas corpus | "to have the body" | produce a detained person; against state and private persons |
| Mandamus | "we command" | orders a public official to do a duty; not against President, Governor, private persons |
| Prohibition | "to forbid" | higher court stops a lower court or tribunal from exceeding jurisdiction (before decision) |
| Certiorari | "to be certified" | quashes an order already passed |
| Quo warranto | "by what authority" | questions a person's claim to a public office |
- SC (Art. 32) issues writs only for FRs; **High Courts (Art. 226)** for FRs and any other legal right - so HC's writ jurisdiction is **wider**.
- Art. 33: Parliament may restrict FRs of armed forces, police. Art. 34: martial law. Art. 31A, 31B (Ninth Schedule), 31C protect certain laws. **I.R. Coelho (2007)**: Ninth Schedule laws added after 24 April 1973 can be tested against basic structure.
Trap: Rights available to foreigners too: 14, 20, 21, 21A, 22, 23, 24, 25-28. Only citizens: 15, 16, 19, 29, 30.

## Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Articles 36-51)
Explanation: FRs tell the State what **not** to do. DPSPs tell the State what it **should** do to build a welfare state: reduce poverty, give work, protect health. Courts cannot enforce them (non-justiciable), but they are "fundamental in the governance of the country" (Art. 37). They are like a to-do list for every government; voters judge the performance.
| Group | Articles and content |
|---|---|
| Socialist | 38 reduce inequality; 39 livelihood, equal pay, no concentration of wealth; **39A free legal aid**; 41 work, education, public assistance; 42 maternity relief; 43 living wage; 43A workers' participation; 47 nutrition and public health |
| Gandhian | **40 village panchayats**; 43 cottage industries; **43B cooperatives**; 46 promote SC/ST interests; 47 prohibition of liquor; 48 ban cow slaughter |
| Liberal-intellectual | **44 Uniform Civil Code**; 45 early childhood care; 48 modern agriculture; **48A environment**; 49 monuments; **50 separate judiciary from executive**; **51 international peace** |
- Added by the 42nd Amendment: 39(f) children's opportunities, 39A, 43A, 48A. By the 44th: 38(2). By the 97th: 43B.
| Basis | Fundamental Rights | DPSP |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | negative - restrain the State | positive - direct the State |
| Enforceability | justiciable | non-justiciable |
| Aim | political democracy | social and economic democracy |
| Beneficiary | individual | community |
- **Conflict**: Champakam Dorairajan (1951) - FRs prevail. Golaknath (1967) - FRs cannot be amended. Kesavananda (1973) - basic structure. **Minerva Mills (1980)** - the Constitution is founded on the **balance** between Part III and Part IV; harmony is basic structure.
- DPSPs implemented: MGNREGA (41), Maternity Benefit Act (42), Panchayati Raj (40), legal services authorities (39A), RTE (45), Environment Act (48A).

## Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Article 51A)
- Added by the **42nd Amendment 1976** on the **Swaran Singh Committee**'s recommendation; source USSR. 10 duties then; the 11th (educate children 6-14) by the 86th Amendment 2002.
- Abide by the Constitution, flag, anthem; cherish ideals of the freedom struggle; uphold sovereignty and unity; defend the country; promote harmony and renounce practices derogatory to women; preserve composite culture; protect environment and have compassion for living creatures; develop **scientific temper**, humanism; safeguard public property and abjure violence; strive for excellence; educate children.
- Only for citizens; non-justiciable, but courts use them to interpret laws and Parliament can enforce them by law (e.g. Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act 1971).

## Amendment of the Constitution (Article 368)
Explanation: A constitution must change with time, but not so easily that a temporary majority can destroy it. India chose a middle path.
| Method | Majority needed | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Simple majority (outside Art. 368) | more than 50% of members present and voting | new states, citizenship, abolition of legislative councils, Second Schedule |
| Special majority | majority of total membership **and** 2/3 of members present and voting, in each House | FRs, DPSPs, most provisions |
| Special majority + ratification by half the states | above + resolutions of half the state legislatures | election of President, SC and HCs, Seventh Schedule lists, representation of states, Art. 368 itself, GST |
- Bill may be introduced in either House, by a minister or private member; no prior permission of the President; **no joint sitting**; the President **must** give assent (24th Amendment).
### Basic Structure doctrine - the single most important idea in Polity
@chain Shankari Prasad 1951: Parliament can amend FRs -> Golaknath 1967: cannot amend FRs -> 24th Amendment 1971 -> Kesavananda Bharati 1973: can amend anything but not the basic structure -> Minerva Mills 1980: limited amending power itself is basic :: Evolution of the doctrine
- Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala: 13-judge bench, 7:6 verdict, 24 April 1973.
- Elements held to be basic structure: supremacy of the Constitution, republican and democratic form, secularism (S.R. Bommai 1994), federalism, separation of powers, judicial review, rule of law, free and fair elections (Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain 1975), independence of judiciary (NJAC struck down 2015), parliamentary system, harmony between FRs and DPSPs, limited amending power.

## Emergency Provisions (Part XVIII, Articles 352-360)
Explanation: In a crisis the federal Constitution can become almost unitary. This is unique to India.
| Point | National Emergency (352) | President's Rule (356) | Financial Emergency (360) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | war, external aggression, **armed rebellion** | failure of constitutional machinery in a state | threat to financial stability |
| Parliament's approval | within **1 month**, special majority | within **2 months**, simple majority | within 2 months, simple majority |
| Duration | 6 months at a time, indefinitely | 6 months; maximum **3 years** | indefinite |
| Effect on FRs | Art. 19 suspended automatically (only for war/external aggression); others by order under 359, except 20 and 21 | none | none |
| Used | 1962, 1971, 1975 | 130+ times | never |
- National Emergency needs the **written advice of the Cabinet**; Lok Sabha can disapprove by simple majority; Lok Sabha's life can be extended by one year at a time.
- **S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)**: President's Rule is subject to judicial review; majority must be tested on the **floor of the House**; assembly cannot be dissolved before Parliament's approval; secularism is basic structure. Sarkaria and Punchhi Commissions: use Art. 356 as a last resort.

## Common Misconceptions
- "The Preamble can be enforced in court" - no.
- "DPSPs are useless because not enforceable" - they guide laws and courts read them into Art. 21.
- "Right to property is abolished" - it is a constitutional legal right (300A), just not fundamental.
- "India has the due process clause" - text says "procedure established by law"; due process came through Maneka Gandhi.

## Mains Answer Practice
Q: "The Indian Constitution is federal in form but unitary in spirit." Discuss. (150 words)
Model answer: Intro - India is a "Union of States" with features of both. Federal features: written and supreme Constitution, division of powers in three lists, independent judiciary, bicameralism. Unitary features: strong centre (residuary powers, Union List larger), single citizenship, integrated judiciary, all-India services, Governor appointed by the centre, emergency provisions, Parliament can redraw states (Art. 3). Assessment: S.R. Bommai held federalism to be basic structure; GST Council and NITI Aayog show cooperative federalism. Conclusion - a flexible federation designed for unity in diversity.
Q: Is the basic structure doctrine a check on democracy or a guardian of it?
Structure: origin -> arguments against (unelected judges, vagueness) -> arguments for (prevented 39th Amendment-type abuse, protects rights) -> conclude: guardian with judicial restraint.
