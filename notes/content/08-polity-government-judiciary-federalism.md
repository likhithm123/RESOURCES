# Polity II - Union, States, Judiciary, Federalism and Institutions
The previous notebook explained the rule book. This one explains the players: who holds power, how they are chosen, how they are removed and how they check each other. Keep one question in mind for every office: **appointment, term, powers, removal**.
## The Parliamentary System in One Picture
@flow (People elect Lok Sabha) | [Party or coalition with majority in Lok Sabha] | [President appoints its leader as Prime Minister] | [PM picks Council of Ministers] | [Ministers run departments with civil servants] | (Lok Sabha can remove the government by no-confidence) :: How a government is formed and held accountable
- **Nominal executive** = President (head of State). **Real executive** = Prime Minister and Council of Ministers (head of Government).
- **Collective responsibility** (Art. 75): the whole Council of Ministers answers to the Lok Sabha; they "swim and sink together".
| Basis | Parliamentary (India, UK) | Presidential (USA) |
|---|---|---|
| Executive | dual: nominal + real | single |
| Membership | ministers are MPs | not members of legislature |
| Tenure | as long as majority lasts | fixed |
| Merit | responsibility, cooperation | stability, expertise |
| Demerit | instability, no strict separation | deadlock, less accountable |
- Why India chose it: familiarity since 1919, preference for accountability over stability, need to represent a diverse society.
## The President (Articles 52-62)
- **Election**: indirect, by an **electoral college** = elected MPs of both Houses + elected MLAs of states + elected MLAs of Delhi and Puducherry. Nominated members and MLCs do not vote. System: proportional representation by single transferable vote, secret ballot.
Formula: Value of one MLA's vote = (population of the state in 1971 census / number of elected MLAs) / 1000. Value of one MP's vote = total value of all MLAs' votes / total elected MPs.
Example: If a state had a 1971 population of 2,08,33,803 and 208 elected MLAs, value of each MLA vote = 20833803 / 208 / 1000 = about 100.
- Qualifications: citizen, 35 years, qualified to be a Lok Sabha member, no office of profit. Term 5 years; re-eligible. Oath by the CJI. Disputes go to the Supreme Court.
- **Impeachment** (Art. 61): only for "violation of the Constitution"; charges signed by 1/4 of a House, 14 days' notice, passed by **2/3 of the total membership** of that House; the other House investigates and passes by the same majority. Nominated MPs take part; MLAs do not. No President has been impeached.
| Power | Examples |
|---|---|
| Executive | appoints PM, ministers, Governors, CJI and judges, CAG, CEC, UPSC, Attorney General; supreme commander |
| Legislative | summons and prorogues Parliament, dissolves Lok Sabha, addresses, nominates **12 to Rajya Sabha**, assents to bills, **ordinances (Art. 123)** |
| Financial | money bills need prior recommendation; lays the budget; Finance Commission |
| Judicial | **pardon (Art. 72)** |
| Emergency | Arts 352, 356, 360 |
- **Art. 74**: President acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers; may send advice back **once** for reconsideration, then must accept (44th Amendment).
### Veto powers
| Veto | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute | withholds assent; bill dies | private members' bills; 1954 PEPSU bill |
| Suspensive | returns bill for reconsideration; if passed again must assent; not for money bills | - |
| Pocket | takes no action; no time limit in the Constitution | Zail Singh and the Indian Post Office Bill 1986 |
- No veto on constitutional amendment bills.
- **Ordinance**: when either House is not in session and immediate action is needed; same force as an Act; must be approved within **6 weeks of reassembly**; maximum life about 6 months + 6 weeks. D.C. Wadhwa (1987) and Krishna Kumar Singh (2017): repeated re-promulgation is a fraud on the Constitution.
| Pardoning term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pardon | removes both sentence and conviction |
| Commutation | lighter form of punishment (death to life) |
| Remission | reduces period, same character |
| Respite | lesser sentence for special fact (pregnancy) |
| Reprieve | temporary stay |
- President can pardon death sentences and court-martial sentences; Governor (Art. 161) cannot pardon court-martial sentences. The power is exercised on cabinet advice and is open to limited judicial review (Maru Ram, Kehar Singh, Epuru Sudhakar).
- **Discretion** of the President: inviting a leader when no party has majority; sending back advice once; pocket veto; dissolving Lok Sabha when the government has lost majority.

## Vice-President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers
- **Vice-President** (Art. 63): elected by **all** members of both Houses (elected + nominated); ex-officio **Chairman of Rajya Sabha**; removed by a Rajya Sabha resolution passed by an effective majority and agreed to by Lok Sabha. Acts as President for at most 6 months.
- **Prime Minister** (Art. 75): appointed by the President; leader of the majority; "keystone of the cabinet arch"; link between President and cabinet (Art. 78); chairs NITI Aayog, National Security Council, Cabinet committees.
- **Council of Ministers**: Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers. Size limit **15% of Lok Sabha strength** (91st Amendment 2003). A minister who is not an MP must become one within **6 months**.
- **Cabinet** = inner core that actually decides policy; the word entered the Constitution only via the 44th Amendment (Art. 352). **Kitchen cabinet** = informal circle. **Cabinet Secretariat** under the Cabinet Secretary coordinates.
- **Attorney General** (Art. 76): first law officer; qualified to be an SC judge; holds office at the President's pleasure; can speak in Parliament but cannot vote.
## Parliament (Articles 79-122)
- Parliament = **President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha**.
| Feature | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum strength | 550 (now 543 elected) | 250 (238 elected + 12 nominated); now 245 |
| Election | direct, first-past-the-post | indirect, by elected MLAs, proportional representation (STV) |
| Term | 5 years | permanent; 1/3 retire every 2 years; member's term 6 years |
| Minimum age | 25 | 30 |
| Presiding officer | Speaker | Vice-President as Chairman |
| Special powers | money bills, no-confidence motion, national emergency disapproval | **Art. 249** (Parliament to legislate on State List), **Art. 312** (create All India Services) |
- Sessions: Budget, Monsoon, Winter; gap between two sessions not more than **6 months**. **Quorum** = 1/10 of total membership.
- **Summon** (call), **adjourn** (suspend a sitting - by presiding officer), **adjourn sine die**, **prorogue** (end a session - by President), **dissolve** (end the life of Lok Sabha - by President).
- **Speaker**: elected by Lok Sabha; decides whether a bill is a **Money Bill** (final), decides disqualification under the Tenth Schedule, has a casting vote, presides over joint sittings; removed by a resolution of **effective majority** with 14 days' notice. Pro tem Speaker administers the oath to new members.
### Types of majorities - a favourite Prelims area
| Majority | Meaning | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Simple | more than 50% of members present and voting | ordinary bills, money bills, no-confidence, President's Rule |
| Absolute | more than 50% of total strength | forming government |
| Effective | more than 50% of effective strength (total minus vacancies) | removal of Speaker, Vice-President (in RS) |
| Special (Art. 368) | 2/3 present and voting + majority of total | constitutional amendments, removal of judges |
| Special (Art. 249, 312) | 2/3 present and voting | Rajya Sabha resolutions |
| Special (Art. 61) | 2/3 of total membership | impeachment of President |
Example: Lok Sabha total 543, vacancies 3, present and voting 400. Simple majority = 201. Effective majority = 271 (of 540). Absolute majority = 272. Special majority under 368 = at least 267 votes (2/3 of 400) and also at least 272.
### How a bill becomes law
@chain First reading (introduction) -> Committee stage -> Second reading (clause-by-clause) -> Third reading (vote) -> Other House -> President's assent -> Act :: Journey of an ordinary bill
| Type | Introduced in | Rajya Sabha's power | Joint sitting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary bill | either House | equal | yes (Art. 108) |
| **Money bill (Art. 110)** | only Lok Sabha, with President's recommendation | can only recommend; must return in **14 days** | no |
| Financial bill (I) (Art. 117(1)) | only Lok Sabha, with recommendation | can amend or reject | yes |
| Constitution amendment bill | either House | equal | no |
- **Money bill** contains **only** matters of tax, borrowing, Consolidated Fund, appropriation, audit. Controversy: Aadhaar Act passed as a money bill (upheld in 2018 by majority; issue referred to a larger bench in Rojer Mathew 2019).
- **Joint sitting**: called by the President, presided by the **Speaker**; held three times - Dowry Prohibition 1961, Banking Service Commission 1978, POTA 2002.
- A bill pending in Lok Sabha lapses on dissolution; a bill pending in Rajya Sabha and not passed by Lok Sabha does not lapse.
### Budget (Art. 112 - Annual Financial Statement)
@chain Presentation (1 Feb) -> General discussion -> Committees examine Demands for Grants -> Voting on demands (cut motions, guillotine) -> Appropriation Bill -> Finance Bill :: Stages of the budget
- **Consolidated Fund** (Art. 266): all revenue, loans; no withdrawal without Parliament's law. **Public Account**: PF, small savings - money held in trust. **Contingency Fund** (Art. 267): for unforeseen expenditure, held by the Finance Secretary on behalf of the President.
- **Charged expenditure** is not voted: salaries of President, judges of SC, CAG, interest on debt.
- Cut motions: policy cut (reduce to Re 1), economy cut (by a specific amount), token cut (by Rs 100). **Vote on account**: advance grant till the budget is passed. Railway budget merged in 2017.
### Tools of parliamentary control
- **Question Hour** (first hour): starred (oral, supplementaries), unstarred (written), short notice. **Zero Hour**: Indian innovation since 1962, not in the rules.
- Motions: adjournment (urgent public matter, needs 50 members, Lok Sabha only), calling attention, **no-confidence** (Lok Sabha only, 50 members' support, no reason needed), censure (must state reasons).
| Committee | Members | Chair | Work |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public Accounts (1921) | 22 (15 LS + 7 RS) | by convention from Opposition since 1967 | examines CAG reports - "post-mortem" |
| Estimates | 30 (all LS) | ruling party | suggests economy - "continuous economy committee" |
| Public Undertakings | 22 | - | PSU performance |
| Departmental Standing Committees (24) | 31 (21 LS + 10 RS) | - | examine demands for grants and bills |
- Ministers cannot be members of these financial committees.
- **Parliamentary privileges** (Art. 105): freedom of speech in the House, no arrest in civil cases 40 days before and after a session. In **Sita Soren (2024)** the Supreme Court held that bribery for a vote or speech is **not** protected, overruling P.V. Narasimha Rao (1998).
### Anti-defection law (Tenth Schedule, 52nd Amendment 1985)
- Disqualification if a member (1) voluntarily gives up party membership, (2) votes against the party whip without permission, (3) an independent joins a party, (4) a nominated member joins a party after 6 months.
- Exception: **merger** with 2/3 of the legislature party (the 1/3 "split" exception was deleted by the 91st Amendment).
- Decided by the Speaker/Chairman; subject to judicial review (**Kihoto Hollohan 1992**). Issues: delay and bias of Speakers (Keisham Meghachandra 2020 suggested 3 months and an independent tribunal), whip curbs dissent.

## The Judiciary
Explanation: India has **one integrated pyramid** of courts. A case can travel from a district court to the High Court to the Supreme Court. The same courts apply both central and state laws (unlike the USA's dual system).
@layers Supreme Court of India | 25 High Courts | District and Sessions Courts | Civil Judge and Magistrate courts, Gram Nyayalayas, Lok Adalats :: Hierarchy of courts
### Supreme Court (Articles 124-147)
- Strength: CJI + **33 judges** (34 total). Qualification: citizen; HC judge for 5 years, or HC advocate for 10 years, or a distinguished jurist. Retire at **65** (HC judges at 62).
- **Appointment - the Collegium story**:
| Case | Outcome |
|---|---|
| First Judges Case (S.P. Gupta 1981) | "consultation" is not concurrence - executive primacy |
| Second Judges Case (1993) | consultation = concurrence; collegium born |
| Third Judges Case (1998) | collegium = CJI + **4** senior-most judges |
| Fourth Judges Case (2015) | 99th Amendment and NJAC struck down - judicial independence is basic structure |
- Removal (Art. 124(4)): on "proved misbehaviour or incapacity"; motion signed by 100 Lok Sabha or 50 Rajya Sabha members -> 3-member inquiry committee -> each House passes with **special majority** in the same session -> President's order. No judge has been removed so far (V. Ramaswami's motion failed in 1993).
| Jurisdiction | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Original (Art. 131) | Centre-state and inter-state disputes - exclusive |
| Writ (Art. 32) | enforcement of FRs |
| Appellate (132-134) | constitutional, civil, criminal appeals |
| Special leave (Art. 136) | discretionary appeal from any court or tribunal (not military) |
| Advisory (Art. 143) | President seeks opinion; not binding |
| Court of record (Art. 129) | records have evidentiary value; power to punish for contempt |
| Review (137), curative petition (Rupa Hurra 2002) | correct its own errors |
| Complete justice (Art. 142) | any order needed to do complete justice |
- **Independence** is secured by: collegium appointment, security of tenure, salaries charged on the Consolidated Fund, no discussion of judges' conduct in legislatures, ban on practice after retirement (SC judges), contempt power, separation from the executive (Art. 50).
### Judicial review, activism and PIL
- **Judicial review** = power to test laws and executive actions against the Constitution (Arts 13, 32, 226). It is basic structure.
- **Judicial activism** = courts take a proactive role to protect rights when other organs fail. **Judicial overreach** = courts entering the policy domain (e.g. banning liquor shops near highways, framing detailed policy).
- **PIL** (Justice P.N. Bhagwati, Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer, early 1980s): relaxed locus standi - any public-spirited person can approach the court for those who cannot. Landmarks: Hussainara Khatoon (undertrials), Bandhua Mukti Morcha (bonded labour), M.C. Mehta (environment), Vishaka (sexual harassment). Criticism: publicity interest litigation, burden on courts.
- Pendency: about 5 crore cases across courts; reasons - vacancies, low judge-population ratio (about 21 per million), adjournments, government as the largest litigant. Remedies: e-Courts, ADR, All India Judicial Service, National Judicial Infrastructure.
- **High Courts** (Arts 214-231): judges appointed by the President; **Art. 226** writs (wider), **Art. 227** superintendence over lower courts. **Subordinate courts** (233-237): district judges appointed by the Governor in consultation with the HC.
- **Tribunals** (Arts 323A, 323B; 42nd Amendment): CAT, NGT, NCLT, ITAT, Armed Forces Tribunal. L. Chandra Kumar (1997): their decisions are subject to HC's writ jurisdiction.
- **ADR**: arbitration, mediation (Mediation Act 2023), conciliation, **Lok Adalats** (Legal Services Authorities Act 1987; award is final, no appeal). NALSA provides free legal aid (Art. 39A).

## State Government
- **Governor** (Arts 153-162): appointed by the President; holds office during the President's pleasure; age 35; no impeachment process. Dual role - constitutional head of the state and agent of the centre.
- **Discretionary powers**: reserving a bill for the President (Art. 200), recommending President's Rule (356), inviting a leader in a hung assembly, acting as administrator of an adjoining UT.
| Case / commission | Point |
|---|---|
| Shamsher Singh (1974) | Governor acts on ministerial advice except where discretion is given |
| Nabam Rebia (2016) | Governor cannot summon the assembly on his own; discretion is limited |
| State of Punjab (2023), State of Tamil Nadu (2025) | Governor cannot sit on bills indefinitely; must act within reasonable time |
| Sarkaria (1988), Punchhi (2010) | eminent non-partisan person, consult the CM, fixed tenure, last-resort use of 356 |
- **Chief Minister** and Council of Ministers (163-164): same principles as the Union; small states minimum 12 ministers. **Advocate General** (165).
- **State legislature**: Legislative Assembly (60-500 members) and, in six states, a **Legislative Council** (UP, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana). Council can be created or abolished by Parliament by simple majority after the Assembly passes a resolution by **special majority** (Art. 169). The Council can delay an ordinary bill at most 4 months; there is no joint sitting in states.
## Federalism and Centre-State Relations
Explanation: Federalism means two levels of government, each supreme in its own sphere, with a court as referee. India's federalism is "holding together" (a big country divided into states for convenience), not "coming together" (USA).
### Legislative relations (Arts 245-255)
| List (Seventh Schedule) | Subjects | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Union List | about 100 | defence, foreign affairs, railways, banking, currency, atomic energy |
| State List | about 61 | police, public order, public health, agriculture, land, local government |
| Concurrent List | about 52 | education, forests, marriage, criminal law, labour, electricity |
- Conflict on a Concurrent subject: Union law prevails (Art. 254) unless the state law got the President's assent. **Residuary powers** with Parliament (Art. 248).
- Parliament can legislate on the State List when: Rajya Sabha resolution (249), national emergency (250), two or more states request (252), to implement treaties (253), President's Rule.
- 42nd Amendment moved five subjects to the Concurrent List: education, forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals, administration of justice.
### Administrative and financial relations
- States must comply with Union laws (256-257); **All India Services** (Art. 312); inter-state water disputes (Art. 262 - tribunals; courts' jurisdiction barred); **Inter-State Council** (Art. 263, set up 1990 after Sarkaria); **Zonal Councils** (statutory, 1956; Home Minister chairs).
- **Finance Commission** (Art. 280): every 5 years; recommends **vertical devolution** (share of states in the divisible pool - 41% under the 15th FC, chaired by N.K. Singh, for 2021-26) and **horizontal** distribution (income distance 45%, population 2011 15%, area 15%, demographic performance 12.5%, forest and ecology 10%, tax effort 2.5%). The 16th FC is chaired by Arvind Panagariya (award period from 2026-27).
- **GST** (101st Amendment, 1 July 2017): destination-based tax; **GST Council** (Art. 279A) - Union Finance Minister as chair; centre has 1/3 weight, states 2/3; decisions need 3/4 majority. Mohit Minerals (2022): Council's recommendations are persuasive, not binding.
- Grants: statutory (Art. 275), discretionary (Art. 282). Issues: cesses and surcharges are outside the divisible pool; GST compensation; centrally sponsored schemes.
- **Cooperative federalism**: NITI Aayog Governing Council, GST Council. **Competitive federalism**: states compete on rankings (ease of doing business, SDG index). **Fiscal federalism**: sharing of money.
- Special provisions: Art. 371-371J (Nagaland 371A, Mizoram 371G, Hyderabad-Karnataka 371J); Art. 370 for J&K abrogated in August 2019 (upheld by the Supreme Court in December 2023).

## Local Government
- **Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957)**: three-tier Panchayati Raj; first in Nagaur, Rajasthan, 2 October 1959. Ashok Mehta (1977): two-tier. G.V.K. Rao, L.M. Singhvi (constitutional status).
| Point | 73rd Amendment (Panchayats) | 74th Amendment (Municipalities) |
|---|---|---|
| Part / Schedule | Part IX, Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects) | Part IXA, Twelfth Schedule (18 subjects) |
| Tiers | village, intermediate, district | Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council, Municipal Corporation |
| Force from | 24 April 1993 (Panchayati Raj Day) | 1 June 1993 |
- Compulsory features: **Gram Sabha**; direct elections; 5-year term, election within 6 months of dissolution; reservation for SC/ST and **not less than 1/3 for women** (many states give 50%); **State Election Commission**; **State Finance Commission** every 5 years; District Planning Committee.
- **PESA 1996** extends Panchayati Raj to Fifth Schedule areas with a powerful Gram Sabha (minor forest produce, land alienation, consent for mining of minor minerals).
- Problems - the "3 Fs": **Funds, Functions, Functionaries** not devolved; sarpanch-pati (proxy) culture; weak own revenue; parallel bodies. Urban: weak mayors, parastatals, poor property-tax collection.
- **Fifth Schedule**: Scheduled Areas in 10 states; Tribes Advisory Council; Governor's special powers. **Sixth Schedule**: Autonomous District Councils in **A**ssam, **M**eghalaya, **T**ripura, **M**izoram (AMTM) with law-making powers.

## Constitutional, Statutory and Executive Bodies
| Body | Article / law | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| Election Commission | Art. 324 | CEC + 2 ECs; CEC removed like an SC judge; 2023 Act: selection by PM, a Union minister, Leader of Opposition |
| UPSC / SPSC | 315-323 | members hold office 6 years or till 65 (62 for state); removed by the President after SC inquiry |

| Finance Commission | 280 | chairman + 4 members |
| GST Council | 279A | - |
| NCSC, NCST, NCBC | 338, 338A, 338B | constitutional commissions |
| Attorney General / Advocate General | 76 / 165 | - |
| NHRC | Protection of Human Rights Act 1993 | statutory; recommendations not binding |
| CIC | RTI Act 2005 | statutory |

Trap: "Constitutional body" = mentioned in the Constitution. "Statutory" = created by an Act. "Executive" = created by a government order.
- **Elections**: Representation of the People Act 1950 (rolls, seats) and 1951 (conduct, disqualification, offences). Reforms: EVM-VVPAT, NOTA (2013), disclosure of criminal records (ADR case 2002), immediate disqualification on conviction of 2 years or more (Lily Thomas 2013), electoral bonds struck down (2024). Debates: simultaneous elections, state funding, criminalisation, freebies, delimitation after the next Census.
- **Political parties** are recognised by the ECI as national or state parties; they are not mentioned in the original Constitution except in the Tenth Schedule. **Pressure groups** influence policy without contesting elections (FICCI, trade unions, farmers' unions).
## Practice MCQs with Explanations
